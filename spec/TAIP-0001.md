> **An Intent Record preserves what an accountable party proposed, requested, instructed, or sought to authorize before later systems accept, execute, reject, modify, or dispute it.**

## Abstract

This document defines the TAIP **Intent Record** and **Intent Transition Record**.

An Intent Record is a finalized, attributable Protocol Object that expresses a bounded intended action and its constraints. It identifies who stands behind the intent, on whose behalf it is expressed, what action is intended, which Authority and Policy context is claimed, when the intent applies, which inputs and dependencies are relevant, and which cryptographic protection covers the statement.

An Intent Record does not by itself prove Acceptance, Authorization, execution, settlement, business correctness, or Legal Validity. Those conclusions require separate evidence and applicable specifications.

An Intent Transition Record adds accountable state when an Intent Record is cancelled, withdrawn, superseded, or annotated. It never rewrites the original finalized intent.

Version `0.1.0` defines the normative logical model and validation behavior needed for review and experimental implementation. Stable wire-format, Canonical Representation, algorithm Registry, schema, and media-type bindings remain Draft dependencies and must be completed before stable `1.0.0` conformance.

---

# 1. Status and Applicability

`TAIP-0001` is the first subject-matter specification in the TAIP series.

It applies when a system creates, exchanges, stores, submits, commits, packages, or verifies evidence that an accountable party expressed an intent concerning a consequential action.

Representative domains include:

- payment and transfer requests;
- purchase and sale instructions;
- invoice approval workflows;
- treasury and cash-management actions;
- account, asset, or beneficiary changes;
- policy or authority changes;
- delegated Agent tasks;
- tool and service invocation requests;
- exception and override requests;
- multi-agent workflow coordination;
- any other action designated accountability-critical by an applicable Trust Profile.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-001 — Specification applicability.** An object claiming conformance as a TAIP Intent Record or Intent Transition Record MUST satisfy this specification and all incorporated normative dependencies.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-002 — Draft claim boundary.** An implementation using version `0.1.0` MUST identify the specification as Draft and MUST NOT present experimental interoperability as stable TAIP conformance.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-003 — Role-scoped claims.** A conformance claim MUST identify whether the implementation acts as an Intent Producer, issuer, signer, parser, validator, resolver, Registry, Commitment Service, packager, or verifier.

# 3. Intent Definition

An **Intent** is a bounded statement that a Protocol Identity proposes, requests, instructs, offers, or seeks authorization for an action under identified conditions.

An **Intent Record** is the finalized Protocol Object carrying that statement.

```text
Principal Objective
        │
        ▼
Intent Formulation
        │
        ▼
Finalized Intent Record
        │
        ├── may be submitted
        ├── may be accepted or rejected
        ├── may be committed
        ├── may be cancelled or superseded
        └── may cause later execution evidence
```

Intent is evidence of what the issuer states was intended. It is not automatic evidence that another party accepted the intent, that the issuer possessed sufficient Authority, or that the action occurred.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-007 — Bounded statement.** An Intent Record MUST express one bounded primary intended action or one explicitly atomic composite action.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-008 — Intent/occurrence separation.** An Intent Record MUST NOT be interpreted as evidence that the intended action was attempted, completed, settled, or otherwise occurred.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-009 — Intent/acceptance separation.** An Intent Record MUST NOT be interpreted as Acceptance by an intended actor, counterparty, executor, service, or financial institution.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-010 — Intent/authorization separation.** The presence, Signature, or structural validity of an Intent Record MUST NOT by itself establish Authorization.

# 4. Scope and Non-Goals

TAIP-0001 defines:

- Intent Record classes;
- participant roles;
- the normative logical information model;
- type, version, identifier, and reference rules;
- intended action and constraint semantics;
- Authority and Policy context;
- temporal validity;
- causal, workflow, and idempotency relationships;
- confidentiality and data-minimization metadata;
- extensions and protected scope;
- finalization, digest, Signature, and lifecycle behavior;
- cancellation, withdrawal, supersession, and annotation through Intent Transition Records;
- validation, Verification, conformance, security, and privacy requirements.

TAIP-0001 does not define:

- Acceptance semantics, which belong to `TAIP-0002`;
- execution and result evidence, which belong to `TAIP-0003`;
- complete Authority, identity, Key Transparency, Hash Chain, Witness, Checkpoint, Preservation, Dispute Pack, or Verification specifications;
- a universal action ontology or business Policy language;
- legal formation of contracts, consent, agency, or fiduciary duty;
- a mandatory transport, database, cloud service, API, or SDK;
- disclosure of hidden model reasoning;
- automatic truth of the issuer's statement.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-011 — Scope preservation.** A TAIP-0001 implementation MUST distinguish Intent semantics from later Acceptance, execution, result, Commitment, Witnessing, Checkpointing, Preservation, and Verification semantics.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-012 — No universal business ontology.** TAIP-0001 MUST NOT require one universal action or Policy ontology; domain vocabularies MAY be incorporated through governed Registries, profiles, or extensions.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-013 — No reasoning disclosure.** Core Intent conformance MUST NOT require private model chain-of-thought or unrestricted internal model state.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-014 — Business-truth boundary.** A valid Intent Record MUST NOT be represented as automatic proof that the intended action is lawful, correct, prudent, enforceable, affordable, or factually justified.

# 5. Roles

TAIP-0001 distinguishes roles because one entity may create bytes, another may stand behind the statement, and another may be expected to act.

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Intent Producer | Component that constructs the Intent Record |
| Issuer | Protocol Identity taking responsibility for the intent statement |
| Principal | Protocol Identity on whose behalf the intent is expressed |
| Requester | Participant initiating the request to formulate the intent |
| Intended Actor | Participant expected or permitted to evaluate or perform the action |
| Subject | Account, asset, instrument, object, policy, or person affected by the intended action |
| Counterparty | Other participant materially involved in the proposed action |
| Beneficiary | Participant expected to receive a defined benefit where applicable |
| Signer | Key-controlling role producing a Signature or proof |
| Resolver | Component resolving identities, keys, Authority, Policy, schemas, and references |
| Verifier | Role evaluating the Intent Record under an explicit Verification Context |

One Protocol Identity may occupy several roles. Role coincidence must be explicit where it affects Authority, conflicts of interest, independence, or interpretation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-015 — Issuer presence.** Every Intent Record MUST identify exactly one logical issuer responsible for the bounded intent statement.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-016 — Principal presence.** Every Intent Record MUST identify exactly one logical principal, even when the principal and issuer are the same Protocol Identity.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-017 — Producer visibility.** The Intent Producer MUST be identified when it differs from the issuer or when producer identity is required by the applicable Trust Profile.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-018 — Intended Actor condition.** An Intent Record MUST identify the Intended Actor when the intent is directed to a specific party, Agent, service, role, or constrained set of actors.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-019 — Role separation.** Producer, issuer, principal, requester, Intended Actor, signer, approver, executor, and verifier MUST NOT be presumed identical merely because one Protocol Identity appears in several fields.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-020 — Signer role.** Every Signature or equivalent proof MUST identify the role in which its signer acts and the protected statement for which that role is asserted.

# 6. Intent Record Classes

The logical property `intent_class` identifies how the issuer frames the intent.

Initial classes are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `proposal` | Presents a possible action for evaluation or negotiation |
| `request` | Requests another participant or system to consider or perform an action |
| `instruction` | Directs an action under claimed Authority |
| `offer` | Presents an action or terms for possible Acceptance |
| `authorization_request` | Requests an Authorization decision before action |
| `delegated_task` | Assigns a bounded task to an Agent or service under claimed delegation |

These values describe the issuer's asserted intent class. They do not determine legal effect.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-021 — Governed class.** `intent_class` MUST use a value defined by this specification or an incorporated governed Registry or extension.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-022 — Class semantics.** A binding or profile MUST NOT treat two Intent Record classes as interchangeable when the difference changes Authority, Acceptance, lifecycle, or Verification behavior.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-023 — Instruction boundary.** The value `instruction` MUST be interpreted as a claim of directive intent and MUST NOT by itself establish that sufficient Authority existed.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-024 — Unknown class.** An unknown critical Intent Record class MUST produce an Unsupported result and MUST NOT be processed as a generic request.

|---|---:|---|

| `participants` | 0..n | Intended Actor, requester, subject, counterparty, beneficiary, or other governed roles |
| `action` | 1 | Structured intended action |
| `authority_context` | 1 | Authority claim and evidence status |
| `policy_context` | 1 | Applicable Policy claim and evidence status |
| `constraints` | 1 | Ordered or explicitly unordered set, possibly empty |
| `inputs` | 1 | Relevant embedded or referenced inputs, possibly empty |

**TAIP-0001-REQ-027 — No implicit security fields.** Transport headers, database columns, session state, URLs, or SDK defaults MUST NOT silently supply a mandatory logical property unless an incorporated binding explicitly protects and maps that property.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-028 — Conditional requirements.** A class, action schema, Trust Profile, or critical extension MAY add mandatory properties but MUST NOT weaken Core TAIP-0001 requirements.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-029 — Unknown properties.** Unknown properties outside a governed extension location MUST produce an explicit validation result according to the binding and MUST NOT silently alter the protected semantic object.

# 9. Intent Record Identifier

`intent_id` is the stable identifier of one finalized Intent Record.

```text
Intent ID
≠ Intent Digest
≠ Workflow ID
≠ Idempotency Key
≠ Transaction ID
≠ Storage Locator
```

The initial logical syntax is a URI-form identifier under a governed namespace. A binding or profile may define permitted schemes.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-035 — Stable identity.** Every finalized Intent Record MUST have exactly one `intent_id` assigned no later than finalization and Signature creation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-036 — Uniqueness scope.** The namespace governing `intent_id` MUST define generation responsibility, uniqueness scope, comparison, normalization, persistence, and collision behavior.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-037 — No content ambiguity.** The same `intent_id` MUST NOT identify different finalized protected content.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-038 — Duplicate handling.** Re-receipt of the same `intent_id` with the same canonical digest MAY be processed idempotently as a duplicate.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-039 — Collision handling.** The same `intent_id` with different canonical content or digest MUST be reported as a conflict and MUST NOT be resolved by overwrite or last-write-wins behavior.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-040 — Identifier/digest separation.** An implementation MUST NOT infer content-digest semantics from the syntax of `intent_id` unless the governing namespace explicitly defines a content-derived identifier.

# 10. Action Model

`action` is the structured statement of what is intended.

The logical action model contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `action_type` | 1 | Governed action classification |
| `target` | 0..n | Typed affected object, account, asset, Policy, service, or participant reference |
| `parameters` | 1 | Governed structured parameters, possibly empty |
| `requested_state_change` | 0..1 | Bounded prior-to-intended state transition |
| `human_summary` | 0..1 | Informative display text |

Domain specifications may define action-specific parameters such as amount, asset, currency, destination, quantity, limit, account, instrument, or approval threshold.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-041 — Governed action type.** `action_type` MUST be defined by an incorporated specification, Registry, profile, or critical extension.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-042 — Structured semantics.** Information required to evaluate the intended action MUST use governed structured properties and MUST NOT depend solely on `human_summary` or other free-form text.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-043 — Target typing.** Every target reference MUST identify its relationship, type or namespace, stable identity, and required integrity expectation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-044 — Parameter schema.** Action parameters MUST identify or bind to the schema and version governing their meaning.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-045 — Composite action.** A composite action MUST define atomicity, component identity, success conditions, partial-failure behavior, and whether Acceptance or execution may apply per component.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-046 — Summary boundary.** `human_summary` MUST be treated as informative unless its exact content is explicitly part of the governed protected semantics.

# 11. Authority Context

`authority_context` states what Authority relationship the issuer claims for the intent and how supporting evidence is provided.

It contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `referenced`, `embedded`, `not_asserted`, `not_applicable`, or `unavailable` |
| `subject` | 0..1 | Identity whose Authority is asserted |
| `grantor` | 0..1 | Identity granting Authority |
| `authority_refs` | 0..n | Typed Authority or delegation evidence |
| `scope` | 0..1 | Permitted action scope and limits |
| `effective_boundary` | 0..1 | Applicable interval or state boundary |
| `key_purpose` | 0..n | Required purpose of signing or action keys |
| `approval_requirements` | 0..n | Required approvals or thresholds |

`not_asserted` means the Intent Record does not claim sufficient Authority. It does not mean Authority is absent. `unavailable` means the record claims relevant Authority context exists but required evidence could not be supplied or resolved.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-047 — Explicit Authority status.** Every Intent Record MUST state one governed `authority_context.status` value.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-048 — Authority evidence.** A status of `referenced` or `embedded` MUST identify Authority evidence sufficient to evaluate subject, grantor, scope, limits, effective boundary, and applicable Policy.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-049 — Authority claim boundary.** `authority_context` MUST distinguish an issuer's Authority claim from a verifier's conclusion that Authority requirements were satisfied.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-050 — Key/Authority separation.** Possession of a signing key, API credential, account session, or service token MUST NOT be treated as sufficient Authority evidence.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-051 — Delegation chain.** Delegated or subdelegated Authority MUST preserve the accountable chain of grantors, subjects, scopes, conditions, effective boundaries, and revocation status required by the claim.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-052 — Missing Authority.** Missing, unavailable, unsupported, expired, revoked, conflicted, or out-of-scope Authority evidence MUST remain explicit and MUST affect Authorization conclusions.

`policy_context` identifies the Policy state relevant to interpretation of the intent.

It contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `referenced`, `embedded`, `not_asserted`, `not_applicable`, or `unavailable` |
| `policy_refs` | 0..n | Typed Policy identifiers, versions, and integrity references |
| `issuer` | 0..1 | Policy authority or publisher |
| `effective_boundary` | 0..1 | Applicable time or state interval |
| `evaluation_refs` | 0..n | Policy-evaluation evidence |
| `exception_refs` | 0..n | Override, waiver, or exception evidence |

**TAIP-0001-REQ-053 — Explicit Policy status.** Every Intent Record MUST state one governed `policy_context.status` value.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-054 — Historical Policy.** Policy references MUST bind to the version and effective boundary applicable to the intent and MUST NOT rely solely on a mutable current Policy.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-055 — Evaluation separation.** Policy presence, successful parsing, or an issuer's Policy assertion MUST NOT be treated as proof that the Policy was correctly evaluated or legally sufficient.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-056 — Override visibility.** A Policy exception, waiver, or override affecting the intended action MUST be explicitly referenced and attributable.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-057 — Missing Policy.** Unavailable, unsupported, invalid, conflicted, or inapplicable mandatory Policy evidence MUST produce an explicit bounded result.

# 13. Constraints and Prohibitions

`constraints` defines bounded conditions on the intended action.

Each constraint contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `constraint_id` | 1 | Stable identifier within the Intent Record |
| `constraint_type` | 1 | Governed semantic class |
| `operator` | 1 | Governed comparison or evaluation operation |
| `value` | 1 | Typed comparison value or reference |
| `unit` | 0..1 | Governed unit where applicable |
| `subject` | 0..1 | Action field, target, participant, or state to which it applies |
| `criticality` | 1 | `mandatory`, `conditional`, `advisory`, or `prohibited` |
| `condition` | 0..1 | Condition activating a conditional constraint |
| `evidence_refs` | 0..n | Evidence required to evaluate satisfaction |

Examples include maximum amount, permitted asset, destination allowlist, time window, approval threshold, jurisdiction, counterparty restriction, execution deadline, price limit, or explicit prohibition.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-058 — Constraint identity.** Each constraint MUST have a unique `constraint_id` within the Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-059 — Governed evaluation.** `constraint_type`, `operator`, value type, units, and comparison behavior MUST be defined by an incorporated normative source.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-060 — Mandatory constraints.** An Intended Actor or verifier MUST NOT silently ignore a mandatory or prohibited constraint.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-061 — Conditional constraints.** A conditional constraint MUST define a deterministically evaluable activation condition or produce an explicit indeterminate or unsupported result.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-062 — Constraint conflict.** Mutually inconsistent or unsatisfiable constraints MUST produce a conflict result and MUST NOT be resolved by arbitrary priority.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-063 — Empty set meaning.** An empty `constraints` collection means that TAIP-0001 expresses no additional protocol constraints; it MUST NOT be interpreted as absence of external Policy, Authority, legal, or operational limits.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-064 — No weakening by extension.** An extension or profile MUST NOT silently weaken a protected mandatory constraint.

# 14. Inputs, Expected Outcomes, and External References

`inputs` identifies evidence or data the issuer declares relevant to formulation or evaluation of the intent.

An input may be:

- embedded structured data;
- a typed reference to another Protocol Object;
- a digest-bound external payload;
- an encrypted attachment;
- a commitment to selectively disclosed data;
- a Policy, Authority, approval, quote, invoice, account state, or prior Intent Record.

`expected_outcomes` describes bounded desired results. It does not assert that those results occurred.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-065 — Input typing.** Each input MUST identify its semantic role, type or schema, identity, and whether it is embedded, external, unavailable, withheld, or selectively disclosed.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-066 — External integrity.** A substitution-sensitive external input MUST bind its expected digest, algorithm, representation, and size or other safe retrieval constraints.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-067 — Locator separation.** A storage or network locator MUST NOT be treated as the identity or integrity proof of an external input.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-068 — Input-truth boundary.** Integrity of an input MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the input is factually correct, complete, current, or authoritative.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-069 — Expected-outcome boundary.** An `expected_outcomes` entry MUST be interpreted as an intended result and MUST NOT be represented as execution or settlement evidence.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-070 — Missing input.** An unavailable or invalid mandatory input MUST remain explicit and MUST affect completeness, Authorization, constraint, or profile conclusions as applicable.

# 15. Temporal Context and Validity

`temporal_context` identifies the time meanings applicable to the Intent Record.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `record_time` | 1 | Issuer-claimed time of finalization |
| `event_time` | 0..1 | Claimed time at which intent was expressed if different |
| `not_before` | 0..1 | Earliest intended reliance boundary |
| `expires_at` | 0..1 | Latest intended reliance boundary |
| `time_source` | 1 | Source or class of the protected time assertion |
| `precision` | 0..1 | Declared precision |
| `uncertainty` | 0..1 | Declared uncertainty interval or semantics |
| `max_uses` | 0..1 | Maximum permitted Acceptance or execution uses where governed |

Time values are issuer claims unless separately supported by Signature, Commitment, Witness, Checkpoint, anchor, or trusted-time evidence.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-071 — Record time.** Every Intent Record MUST contain a protected `record_time` with governed syntax and declared time semantics.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-072 — Time-source visibility.** Every protected time value MUST identify or resolve its source, precision, and uncertainty when those properties affect interpretation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-073 — Validity ordering.** When both are present, `not_before` MUST be earlier than `expires_at`; otherwise temporal validation MUST fail.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-074 — Not-yet-valid result.** Reliance before `not_before` MUST produce a distinct not-yet-valid result rather than an ordinary valid result.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-075 — Expiry result.** Reliance after `expires_at` MUST produce a distinct expired result unless a governed historical evaluation is determining whether reliance occurred before expiry.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-076 — No backdating inference.** An early `event_time` or `record_time` MUST NOT be treated as proof that the Intent Record existed, was signed, submitted, accepted, or committed at that time.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-077 — Use limit.** A `max_uses` constraint MUST identify the event counted as a use and the evidence required to determine prior uses; inability to determine the count MUST remain explicit.

# 16. Relationships, Workflow, Retry, and Idempotency

`relationships` connects the Intent Record to other governed objects and workflow state.

Initial relationship types include:

- `derived_from`;
- `responds_to`;
- `requested_by`;
- `authorized_by`;
- `depends_on`;
- `retries`;
- `supersedes`;
- `replaces`;
- `related_to`.

Each relationship identifies the target object, type, stable ID, expected digest where required, and semantic strength.

Workflow, correlation, business-case, transaction, attempt, and idempotency identifiers remain distinct.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-078 — Typed relationship.** Every accountability-relevant relationship MUST use governed semantics and MUST identify the target strongly enough for the applicable claim.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-079 — Causality boundary.** Correlation, shared workflow membership, timing, or storage proximity MUST NOT be treated as causal evidence without a governed causal relationship.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-080 — Idempotency separation.** An idempotency key MUST NOT replace `intent_id` and MUST NOT by itself prove that two submissions carry identical intent.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-081 — Retransmission.** Retransmission of the same finalized Intent Record MUST preserve the same `intent_id` and canonical digest.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-082 — New attempt.** A materially new attempt, changed action, changed constraint, changed Authority context, or changed validity interval MUST use a new Intent Record and new `intent_id`.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-083 — Retry linkage.** A new Intent Record representing a retry SHOULD reference the prior Intent Record using a governed `retries` relationship.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-084 — Concurrent conflict.** Conflicting concurrent intents affecting an exclusive action or shared state MUST remain visible and MUST NOT be resolved solely by arrival order.

| `profile_id` | 0..1 | Stable Trust Profile identifier |
| `profile_version` | 0..1 | Exact or governed compatible version |
| `required_controls` | 0..n | Intent-specific control references if permitted by the profile |

**TAIP-0001-REQ-085 — Trust-context status.** Every Intent Record MUST identify whether an Intended Trust Profile is specified, not specified, or unavailable.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-086 — Protected profile reference.** A specified profile identifier and version MUST be cryptographically protected when they affect processing or assurance.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-087 — Intended/Achieved separation.** A specified Intended Trust Profile MUST NOT be treated as proof of the Achieved Trust Profile.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-088 — Profile non-redefinition.** A Trust Profile MAY add requirements or prohibit options but MUST NOT redefine TAIP-0001 Core semantics incompatibly.

# 18. Confidentiality and Data Minimization

`confidentiality` declares handling and disclosure semantics for the Intent Record and its dependencies.

It may identify:

- classification;
- permitted recipient classes;
- encryption requirement;
- payload separation;
- disclosure purpose;
- redaction policy;
- retention category;
- jurisdictional or contractual handling tags;
- metadata sensitivity.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-089 — Minimum sufficient evidence.** An Intent Record MUST contain or reference evidence sufficient for its Accountability Claim while minimizing unrelated personal, confidential, proprietary, and operational data.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-090 — Protected classification.** Confidentiality metadata affecting handling or disclosure MUST be inside the protected scope or integrity-bound by a governed packaging mechanism.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-091 — Encryption boundary.** Encryption or access control MUST NOT replace integrity, issuer attribution, Authority evaluation, or explicit completeness status.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-092 — Redaction visibility.** Redacted or selectively disclosed Intent Records MUST remain distinguishable from complete original records and MUST identify the derivation and omitted protected scope.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-093 — Metadata privacy.** Implementations MUST evaluate linkability and disclosure risks arising from participant IDs, workflow IDs, timestamps, targets, constraints, Authority references, and retrieval metadata.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-094 — Privacy limitation.** A verifier unable to access protected mandatory material MUST report the resulting limitation without treating the material as successfully evaluated.

# 19. Extensions

`extensions` is the only Core location for additional Intent Record semantics not defined by an incorporated action schema or profile.

Each extension identifies:

- namespace and stable ID;
- version;
- authority;
- criticality;
- permitted location and scope;
- value schema;
- Canonical Representation treatment;
- protected scope;
- validation and Verification behavior;
- compatibility and deprecation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-095 — Extension identity.** Every extension MUST have a governed namespace, stable identifier, version, and authority.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-096 — Critical extension.** An unknown critical extension MUST produce Unsupported for every conclusion whose meaning may depend on it.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-097 — Non-critical extension.** An unknown non-critical extension MAY be preserved and ignored only when ignoring it cannot change mandatory interpretation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-098 — Protected extension semantics.** Substitution-sensitive extension identity, version, criticality, and value MUST be cryptographically protected.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-099 — No Core redefinition.** An extension MUST NOT redefine the meaning of a Core TAIP-0001 property, class, state, constraint, or result.

# 20. Canonical Representation and Digest

A finalized Intent Record participating in identifier derivation, digesting, signing, Commitment, or proof generation requires deterministic canonical input.

The canonical protected object includes every normative logical property except `proofs` and explicitly unprotected transport, storage, cache, and presentation metadata.

Conceptually:

```text
canonical_input =
    domain_separator
    || canonicalize(protected_intent_object_without_proofs)

intent_digest =
    digest(algorithm, canonical_input)
```

The domain separator must bind at least TAIP, the `taip.intent` object type, object version, and canonicalization method.

Version `0.1.0` does not designate a stable mandatory JSON canonicalization and cryptographic suite. Experimental bindings must identify their exact canonicalization and digest methods and must not claim stable cross-binding conformance.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-100 — Deterministic input.** An incorporated binding MUST define deterministic canonical bytes for every valid protected Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-101 — Domain separation.** Canonical input MUST use domain separation sufficient to prevent substitution across object types, versions, algorithms, and protocol contexts.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-102 — Protected semantics.** Type, version, `intent_id`, class, issuer, principal, action, Authority, Policy, constraints, temporal context, relationships, trust context, confidentiality, and critical extensions MUST be inside the protected scope.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-103 — Exclusion visibility.** Every property excluded from canonical input MUST be identified by the binding and MUST NOT carry substitution-sensitive normative meaning.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-104 — Digest metadata.** A represented Intent digest MUST identify the digest algorithm, parameters or suite, canonicalization method, domain, and target object version.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-105 — Digest boundary.** A matching Intent digest MUST NOT be interpreted as issuer attribution, Authority, Acceptance, Commitment, Completeness, or execution evidence.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-106 — Non-finite and ambiguous values.** A binding MUST reject duplicate property names, ambiguous number forms, non-finite numbers, invalid Unicode, and other representations that prevent deterministic canonicalization.

# 21. Signatures and Proofs

`proofs` contains one or more cryptographic proofs over the finalized protected Intent Record.

Each proof identifies:

| Property | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `proof_type` | Signature or other approved proof class |
| `signer` | Signer Protocol Identity |
| `role` | Issuer, principal, producer, approver, or other governed role |
| `key_id` | Key identifier |
| `key_purpose` | Purpose under which the proof was created |
| `algorithm` | Algorithm, suite, and parameters |
| `canonicalization` | Protected-input method |
| `target_digest` | Digest of the protected Intent Record |
| `claimed_time` | Signer-claimed proof time, if present |
| `value` | Cryptographic proof value |

At least one proof must support issuer attribution for a conforming finalized Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-107 — Issuer proof.** Every finalized Intent Record MUST contain or integrity-bind at least one proof whose declared role supports issuer attribution.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-108 — Proof target.** Every proof MUST bind the exact protected Intent digest, object type, version, algorithm context, and proof purpose.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-109 — Signer identity.** A proof MUST identify or resolve the signer, key, Key Purpose, and applicable historical identity-to-key relationship.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-110 — Historical verification.** Proof verification MUST use the key status, identity binding, Key Purpose, Authority, algorithm policy, and specification versions applicable to the relevant historical boundary.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-111 — Signature/Authority separation.** Cryptographic proof validity MUST be reported separately from issuer Authority, principal Authorization, Policy compliance, Intent validity, and business effect.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-112 — Multiple proofs.** Multiple proofs MUST be evaluated by role, scope, order, independence, Key Purpose, and applicable threshold rules rather than simple count.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-113 — Unsupported proof.** An unsupported, malformed, ambiguous, invalid, conflicted, or unavailable mandatory proof MUST produce an explicit non-success result.

# 22. Finalization and Lifecycle

A mutable draft is not a TAIP Intent Record.

Finalization establishes the protected logical content intended for identifier assignment, canonicalization, digesting, signing, Submission, reference, or Commitment.

```text
Local Draft
    │ validate and finalize
    ▼
Finalized Intent Record
    │ protect
    ▼
Signed Intent Record
    │ submit
    ▼
Submitted Intent Record
    │ separate receiver evidence
    ▼
Accepted or Rejected
    │ separate historical evidence
    ▼
Committed / Witnessed / Checkpointed / Preserved
```

The lifecycle dimensions are distinct. A record may be finalized and signed without being submitted. It may be submitted without being accepted. It may be accepted without being executed or historically committed.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-114 — Draft distinction.** Mutable drafts MUST NOT be assigned the same conformance status as finalized Intent Records.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-115 — Finalized immutability.** Protected content MUST NOT be modified in place after finalization, digest generation, signing, or protected reference by another object.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-116 — New content identity.** A change to finalized protected content MUST create a new Intent Record with a new `intent_id` or an explicitly defined content-derived identity according to the governing namespace.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-117 — Submission evidence.** Submission MUST be evidenced separately from local finalization and Signature creation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-118 — Acceptance evidence.** Acceptance or rejection MUST be represented by evidence conforming to `TAIP-0002` or an explicitly incorporated equivalent and MUST NOT be inferred solely from transport success.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-119 — Commitment evidence.** Historical Commitment MUST be supported by a governed Commitment Receipt, Chain Entry, or equivalent evidence and MUST NOT be inferred from storage success.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-120 — State exactness.** An operation or API response MUST expose the exact lifecycle state it establishes and MUST NOT return a stronger state label than its evidence supports.

# 23. Temporal Intent State

Intent state is evaluated at a specific time and historical boundary.

Possible evaluated states include:

- `not_yet_applicable`;
- `applicable`;
- `expired`;
- `cancelled`;
- `withdrawn`;
- `superseded`;
- `conflicted`;
- `indeterminate`.

These are evaluation results, not mutable fields written back into the original Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-121 — Contextual state.** Intent state MUST be derived from the original record, validity interval, transition evidence, applicable versions, and Verification Context.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-122 — No mutable status.** An implementation MUST NOT rewrite a finalized Intent Record's protected status field to represent cancellation, expiry, withdrawal, or supersession.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-123 — Historical state.** A later cancellation, expiry, or supersession MUST NOT make the Intent Record appear absent or inapplicable at an earlier historical boundary where it was applicable.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-124 — Conflict visibility.** Concurrent or incompatible transition evidence MUST produce a conflicted or indeterminate state until governed resolution evidence is available.

# 24. Intent Transition Record

An **Intent Transition Record** adds accountable state about an existing Intent Record.

For this version:

```text
object_type    = taip.intent.transition
object_version = 0.1.0
```

The logical model contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `object_type` | 1 | Fixed `taip.intent.transition` |
| `object_version` | 1 | Transition semantic version |
| `taip_context` | 1 | Applicable TAIP versions |
| `transition_id` | 1 | Stable transition identifier |
| `transition_type` | 1 | `cancel`, `withdraw`, `supersede`, or `annotate` |
| `target_intent` | 1 | Target `intent_id` and expected digest |
| `replacement_intent` | 0..1 | Required for `supersede` |
| `issuer` | 1 | Identity responsible for the transition statement |
| `authority_context` | 1 | Authority for the transition |
| `policy_context` | 1 | Applicable Policy and exceptions |
| `effective_time` | 1 | Claimed transition boundary |
| `reason` | 0..1 | Governed reason code and optional explanation |
| `relationships` | 1 | Supporting and causal references, possibly empty |
| `extensions` | 1 | Governed extension set, possibly empty |
| `canonicalization` | 1 | Canonical method and version |
| `proofs` | 1..n | Transition issuer proofs |

Transition meanings are:

- `cancel` — requests that future reliance or action cease from the effective boundary;
- `withdraw` — withdraws the issuer's continuing proposal, request, offer, or instruction;
- `supersede` — identifies a new Intent Record as the replacement for future reliance;
- `annotate` — adds explanation without changing applicability by itself.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-125 — New transition object.** Cancellation, withdrawal, supersession, or annotation of a finalized Intent Record MUST use a new protected Intent Transition Record or another explicitly incorporated append-only object.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-126 — Strong target binding.** `target_intent` MUST bind both the target `intent_id` and expected canonical digest unless the identifier is governed as content-derived.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-127 — Transition authority.** A state-changing transition MUST identify Authority evidence sufficient to evaluate whether the issuer could alter future reliance on the target intent.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-128 — Supersession target.** A `supersede` transition MUST identify the replacement Intent Record by stable ID and digest.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-129 — No content mutation.** A transition MUST NOT alter the bytes, digest, Signature, or historical meaning of the target Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-130 — Effective boundary.** A transition MUST identify its claimed effective time and MUST NOT imply historical effect earlier than evidence and governing rules support.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-131 — Past action boundary.** Cancellation, withdrawal, or supersession MUST NOT by itself invalidate an Acceptance or execution that occurred before the effective transition boundary.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-132 — Transition conflict.** Conflicting authorized transitions MUST remain visible and MUST be evaluated under explicit ordering, Authority, Policy, and historical Commitment rules.

# 25. Boundary with Acceptance and Execution

TAIP-0001, TAIP-0002, and TAIP-0003 represent distinct statements.

| Object | Principal statement |
|---|---|
| Intent Record | “The issuer expresses this bounded intent.” |
| Acceptance Evidence | “The accepting party accepted, rejected, deferred, or conditioned this intent.” |
| Execution Evidence | “The executor attempted or performed this action and reports this result.” |

```text
Intent
   │ may cause
   ▼
Acceptance / Rejection
   │ may authorize or initiate
   ▼
Execution Attempt
   │ may produce
   ▼
Result / Settlement Evidence
```

The arrows are typed causal relationships, not automatic state transitions.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-133 — Acceptance target.** Acceptance Evidence MUST identify the exact Intent Record ID and digest to which it responds.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-134 — Conditional Acceptance.** An Acceptance that modifies action, constraints, validity, Authority, or Policy MUST remain distinguishable from Acceptance of the original intent and MAY require a new Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-135 — Execution target.** Execution Evidence MUST identify the Intent and Acceptance evidence whose scope it claims to implement, where those objects are required by the applicable workflow.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-136 — No implied execution.** Acceptance MUST NOT be interpreted as execution, and execution request transport success MUST NOT be interpreted as completed execution.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-137 — Result independence.** An external result referenced by an Intent Record remains an input or expected result and MUST NOT be represented as independently observed execution evidence.

# 26. Validation Procedure

A TAIP-0001 validator evaluates layers in an order that preserves precise failure information.

## 26.1 Processing Order

1. enforce resource limits and decode the binding;
2. detect duplicate or forbidden representation features;
3. resolve object type, version, schema, and critical extensions;
4. validate required logical properties and cardinalities;
5. construct Canonical Representation and compute digest;
6. verify proof syntax and cryptographic validity;
7. resolve signer identities, keys, Key Purpose, and Historical Key State;
8. evaluate issuer and principal relationships;
9. resolve Authority and Policy evidence;
10. validate action type, parameters, targets, constraints, and units;
11. evaluate time, validity, expiry, and use limits;
12. validate relationships, inputs, and Verification Dependencies;
13. resolve Intent Transition Records and historical ordering;
14. evaluate Intended Trust Profile requirements;
15. compute dimensioned results and limitations.

An implementation may optimize execution but must preserve equivalent outcomes and dependency visibility.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-138 — Layered validation.** Validation MUST distinguish representation, structure, semantics, cryptography, identity, Authority, Policy, temporal, relationship, lifecycle, completeness, and profile results.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-139 — Resource limits.** Processing MUST enforce configured or profile-defined limits for size, nesting, collection length, references, decompression, cryptographic work, and resolver activity.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-140 — No partial-success masking.** Success in one validation layer MUST NOT conceal failure, unsupported behavior, or unavailable mandatory evidence in another layer.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-141 — Dependency graph.** The validator MUST identify every mandatory external dependency and its resolution status.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-142 — Historical resolution.** Identity, key, Authority, Policy, Registry, profile, transition, and specification state MUST be resolved for the relevant historical boundary.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-143 — Cycle handling.** Cyclic or recursively unbounded relationships MUST produce a bounded explicit result and MUST NOT cause uncontrolled processing.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-144 — Deterministic comparison.** Equality, identifier comparison, constraint evaluation, time comparison, and digest comparison MUST use governed deterministic rules.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-145 — Validation evidence.** A validator SHOULD retain or produce evidence sufficient to explain which rules, dependencies, and inputs determined its result.

# 27. Intent Verification Result

TAIP-0001 defines domain-specific result dimensions. TAIP-0010 will define the common Verification Report and composition model.

| Dimension | Representative results |
|---|---|
| Representation | valid, invalid, unsupported |
| Structure | valid, invalid, incomplete |
| Canonicalization | valid, invalid, unsupported |
| Cryptography | valid, invalid, unsupported, unavailable |
| Issuer attribution | established, not established, indeterminate |
| Authority | satisfied, not satisfied, not asserted, unavailable, indeterminate |
| Policy | satisfied, not satisfied, not asserted, unavailable, indeterminate |
| Temporal | applicable, not yet applicable, expired, indeterminate |
| Lifecycle | current, cancelled, withdrawn, superseded, conflicted, indeterminate |
| Constraints | satisfied, not satisfied, unsupported, indeterminate |

**TAIP-0001-REQ-147 — Overall result scope.** Any overall Intent result MUST identify the composition rule, Verification Context, claim, versions, evidence scope, and limitations.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-149 — Incomplete distinction.** Incomplete evidence MUST remain distinguishable from an invalid Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-150 — Historical conclusion.** A historical evaluation MUST state the evaluated time or Commitment boundary and MUST NOT substitute later state silently.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-151 — Business boundary.** A successful Intent Verification result MUST NOT be represented as Acceptance, execution, settlement, legal consent, or business correctness.

# 28. Portability, Packaging, and Preservation

An Intent Record should remain independently verifiable outside the producer's live system.

Portable evidence may include or reference:

- the original finalized Intent Record;
- Canonical Representation and digest metadata;
- Signatures and signer-role context;
- identities, Historical Key State, Authority, and Policy evidence;
- action schemas and Registry state;
- Intent Transition Records;
- Submission, Acceptance, Commitment, Witness, Checkpoint, and anchor evidence;
- Intended Trust Profile and Verification Dependencies;
- redaction, encryption, and disclosure metadata;
- exact specification and binding versions.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-152 — Exportability.** A conforming producer or custodian SHOULD support export of the Intent Record and available required dependencies in a form usable by an authorized independent verifier.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-153 — Original preservation.** Packaging, migration, redaction, or format conversion MUST NOT overwrite or masquerade as the complete original finalized Intent Record.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-154 — Dependency preservation.** Preservation scope MUST include or bind every schema, Registry snapshot, canonicalization rule, key state, Authority, Policy, profile, transition, and specification version required for future interpretation.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-155 — Package boundary.** Valid package structure or package integrity MUST NOT be interpreted as Intent Validity, evidence Completeness, or Trust Profile achievement.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-156 — Unavailable dependency.** A preserved package MUST identify dependencies that are external, unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, unsupported, or not evaluated.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-157 — Migration relationship.** A migrated Intent representation MUST bind the source and target digests, transformation method, tool and version, authority, time, validation result, and information loss.

# 29. Conformance

TAIP-0001 conformance is role-specific.

## 29.1 Producer Conformance

A conforming producer creates complete logical objects, assigns stable IDs, applies required schemas and constraints, finalizes protected content, and emits issuer proofs.

## 29.2 Parser Conformance

A conforming parser recognizes the binding and exposes every protected logical property without silent coercion or loss.

## 29.3 Validator Conformance

A conforming validator evaluates structural, semantic, canonicalization, cryptographic, identity, Authority, Policy, temporal, relationship, and lifecycle rules within its declared capability scope.

## 29.4 Transition Producer Conformance

A conforming transition producer creates append-only, strongly target-bound, authorized transition evidence.

## 29.5 Verifier Conformance

A conforming verifier evaluates available evidence under an explicit context and emits dimensioned bounded results.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-158 — Declared role.** Every TAIP-0001 conformance claim MUST identify the implemented role and exact specification, schema, canonicalization, Registry, profile, binding, and test-suite versions.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-159 — Positive tests.** Conformance tests MUST cover valid Intent Records for every supported class, role combination, action schema, Authority status, Policy status, validity form, extension mode, and proof suite.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-160 — Negative tests.** Conformance tests MUST cover missing fields, duplicate IDs, ID collisions, unknown critical semantics, malformed references, invalid canonicalization, invalid proofs, Authority failure, Policy failure, time errors, constraint conflicts, transition conflicts, and resource limits.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-161 — Boundary tests.** Tests MUST distinguish Intent from Acceptance, execution, Commitment, current state, and business truth.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-162 — Cross-implementation tests.** Canonicalization, digest, Signature input, identifier comparison, temporal evaluation, and result composition MUST be tested across independent implementations before stable release.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-163 — Test traceability.** Every normative `TAIP-0001-REQ-*` rule MUST map to one or more test assertions or an explicit justified non-testable disposition.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-164 — Scoped claim.** Passing a parser or producer subset MUST NOT be represented as full TAIP-0001 verifier or deployment conformance.

**TAIP-0001-REQ-165 — Report evidence.** A conformance report MUST identify executed, passed, failed, skipped, unsupported, and not-applicable tests together with environment, configuration, implementation build, and known limitations.

# 33. Security Considerations

## 33.1 Intent Forgery

An attacker may create or alter an Intent Record to make an action appear requested by another party. Protected issuer identity, deterministic canonicalization, Signatures, Historical Key State, and Authority evaluation are required defenses.

## 33.2 Replay and Duplicate Execution

A valid old intent may be replayed. `intent_id`, validity intervals, `max_uses`, idempotency evidence, Acceptance history, execution history, and transition state must be evaluated together. A valid Signature does not make repeated execution permitted.

## 33.3 Constraint Stripping

An attacker may remove amount limits, destinations, expiry, approval requirements, or prohibited conditions while preserving an unprotected display summary. All substitution-sensitive action and constraint semantics must be cryptographically protected.

## 33.4 Authority Confusion

A signer may possess a valid key without Authority for the action. Verification must resolve Key Purpose, delegation, limits, Policy, approvals, revocation, and historical state separately.

## 33.5 Intent Substitution

Acceptance or Execution Evidence may reference a different intent with the same business correlation value. Strong references must bind `intent_id` and digest.

## 33.6 Time Manipulation

Producer clocks may be wrong or malicious. Event, record, Signature, Submission, Commitment, Witness, and Checkpoint times remain distinct. Historical reliance should prefer stronger external time evidence where required by the profile.

## 33.7 Cancellation Race

A cancellation may race with Acceptance or execution. The result depends on protected effective time, receipt, Commitment order, Authority, Policy, and domain rules. Last database write is not a sufficient protocol rule.

## 33.8 Ambiguous Amounts and Units

Floating-point values, locale-formatted numbers, missing currencies, and inconsistent unit conversions can change action meaning. Domain schemas should use exact representations and governed units.

## 33.9 Reference and Resolver Attacks

External references may redirect, return substituted content, expose query metadata, or create cycles and resource exhaustion. Resolvers require integrity checks, type and size limits, cycle detection, caching rules, and privacy controls.

## 33.10 Parser Differential

Duplicate fields, Unicode normalization, unknown properties, number formats, time parsing, and extension handling can produce cross-implementation differences. Stable bindings require deterministic rejection and canonicalization rules plus cross-implementation vectors.

## 33.11 Overclaiming

The strongest semantic attack is treating a valid signed intent as accepted, authorized, executed, or legally binding. Verifiers and user interfaces must preserve the bounded claim.

---

# 34. Privacy Considerations

Intent evidence can expose financial plans before execution, including amounts, counterparties, beneficiaries, accounts, assets, timing, internal limits, Policy, organizational relationships, and Agent responsibilities.

Implementations should minimize this exposure through:

- commitments or digests instead of unnecessary plaintext;
- separated encrypted payloads;
- selective disclosure;
- role-limited access;
- short-lived locators;
- privacy-aware identity references;
- minimized workflow and correlation identifiers;
- disclosure-specific Dispute Packs;
- protected resolver requests;
- retention appropriate to claim and profile.

Metadata may remain sensitive even when action parameters are encrypted. Timing, issuer, principal, intended actor, profile, Authority references, and transition history can reveal commercial activity.

Privacy restrictions do not authorize silent completeness. A verifier unable to access required information must report the limitation.

---

# 35. Operational Considerations

Implementations must account for:

- offline and delayed intent creation;
- asynchronous Submission and Acceptance;
- clock skew and uncertain expiry;
- network retry and duplicate delivery;
- partial batch processing;
- resolver and key-service outages;
- cancellation races;
- concurrent conflicting intents;
- action-specific schema evolution;
- key rotation between issuance and Verification;
- long-term preservation of expired or superseded intents;
- user-interface wording that does not overstate lifecycle state.

A system may choose fail-closed, fail-open, queue, or manual-review behavior according to its Trust Profile and Policy. The resulting action and achieved assurance must remain explicit.

Operational logs may support diagnosis but do not replace the protected Intent Record or its lifecycle evidence.

# 39. Summary

TAIP-0001 defines a portable and attributable statement of intent for consequential AI-driven actions.

An Intent Record identifies:

- the issuer and principal;
- the intended action and participants;
- Authority and Policy context;
- constraints, inputs, expected outcomes, and time boundaries;
- causal, workflow, retry, and Trust Profile context;
- confidentiality and extension semantics;
- deterministic protected scope and issuer proofs.

An Intent Transition Record adds cancellation, withdrawal, supersession, or annotation without rewriting the original intent.

The central boundary is:

```text
Intent
≠ Authority
≠ Authorization
≠ Acceptance
≠ Execution
≠ Result
≠ Commitment
```

TAIP-0001 makes the first part of an accountable workflow independently inspectable: what was intended, by whom, on whose behalf, under which claimed constraints, and with what evidence.

The next specification, `TAIP-0002`, defines how another accountable party accepts, rejects, conditions, or defers that intent.
