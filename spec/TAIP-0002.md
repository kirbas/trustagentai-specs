## Abstract

This document defines the TrustAgentAI Interoperability Protocol (TAIP) **Acceptance Record** and **Acceptance Transition Record**.

An Acceptance Record is a finalized, attributable Protocol Object through which an accountable responder accepts, conditionally accepts, rejects, or defers an exact [Intent Record](TAIP-0001.md). It identifies the responder, the principal represented by that responder, the accepted or rejected scope, applicable acceptance rules, conditions, Authority and Policy context, temporal boundaries, submission context, and cryptographic protection.

Acceptance is a distinct lifecycle statement. It is stronger than mere transport receipt but does not by itself establish Authorization, execution, result, settlement, Commitment, Witnessing, or Legal Validity.

An Acceptance Transition Record adds accountable state when an earlier Acceptance Record is withdrawn, superseded, expired, corrected, or annotated. It never rewrites the original finalized response.

Version `0.1.0` defines the normative logical model and validation behavior needed for review and experimental implementation. Stable wire-format, Canonical Representation, algorithm Registry, schema, media-type, and common-envelope bindings remain Draft dependencies and must be completed before stable `1.0.0` conformance.

---

# 1. Status and Applicability

`TAIP-0002` is the second subject-matter specification in the TAIP series.

It applies when a person, organization, Agent, workflow, service, counterparty, execution system, Evidence Registry, or other accountable receiver must create portable evidence of its response to an Intent Record.

Representative uses include:

- accepting or rejecting a payment request;
- conditionally accepting a treasury instruction;
- deferring a purchase or invoice decision pending approval;
- acknowledging a delegated Agent task under governed rules;
- accepting only identified components of a composite intent;
- declining an action because Authority, Policy, data, capacity, or timing requirements are not satisfied;
- recording a receiver decision before later execution or Commitment;
- preserving a response across organizational and vendor boundaries.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-001 — Specification applicability.** An object claiming conformance as a TAIP Acceptance Record or Acceptance Transition Record MUST satisfy this specification and all incorporated normative dependencies.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-002 — Draft claim boundary.** An implementation using version `0.1.0` MUST identify the specification as Draft and MUST NOT present experimental interoperability as stable TAIP conformance.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-003 — Role-scoped claims.** A conformance claim MUST identify whether the implementation acts as an Acceptance Producer, issuer, signer, parser, validator, resolver, state evaluator, packager, or verifier.

# 3. Acceptance Definition

**Acceptance** is an accountable receiver's protected response to a specific submitted Intent under identified acceptance rules.

An **Acceptance Record** is the finalized Protocol Object carrying that response.

```text
Finalized Intent Record
        │ submit
        ▼
Receiver Evaluation
        │
        ├── accepted
        ├── conditionally_accepted
        ├── rejected
        └── deferred
                │
                ▼
        Finalized Acceptance Record
```

Acceptance is evidence of what the responder states it accepted, rejected, or deferred. It is not automatic evidence that the responder possessed sufficient Authority, that all conditions were satisfied, that execution was permitted, or that the action occurred.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-007 — Bounded definition.** A conforming Acceptance Record MUST express exactly one governed top-level response to exactly one strongly bound Intent Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-008 — Exact target.** Acceptance MUST bind to the finalized protected content of the target Intent and MUST NOT apply merely to a mutable locator, display label, workflow correlation value, or human summary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-009 — Lifecycle separation.** A valid Acceptance Record MUST NOT be represented as proof of Authorization, execution, result, settlement, Commitment, Witnessing, Checkpointing, Preservation, Verification, or Legal Validity.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-010 — Responder assertion.** Acceptance establishes an attributable responder assertion; it does not make every assertion in the target Intent or response factually true.

# 4. Scope and Non-Goals

TAIP-0002 defines:

- Acceptance Record semantics and required logical properties;
- strong binding to an exact Intent Record;
- acceptance decision classes and scoped responses;
- conditions, reasons, and deferral semantics;
- responder, principal, Authority, and Policy context;
- temporal and submission context;
- deterministic protection and proof requirements;
- append-only Acceptance transitions;
- validation, Verification results, and conformance boundaries.

TAIP-0002 does not define:

- a universal business-contract or legal-offer model;
- a universal Authorization engine;
- execution-system behavior or settlement finality;
- Evidence Registry Commitment or Hash Chain semantics;
- transport delivery guarantees;
- one universal Policy or action ontology;
- disclosure of private model chain-of-thought;
- automatic legal enforceability in any jurisdiction.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-011 — Scope preservation.** A TAIP-0002 implementation MUST distinguish semantic Acceptance from transport receipt, Authorization, execution, result, Commitment, and Verification.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-012 — No universal business ontology.** TAIP-0002 MUST NOT require one universal contract, action, rejection, or Policy ontology; governed domain vocabularies MAY be incorporated through Registries, profiles, or extensions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-013 — No reasoning disclosure.** Core Acceptance conformance MUST NOT require private model chain-of-thought or unrestricted internal model state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-014 — Business-truth boundary.** A valid Acceptance Record MUST NOT be represented as automatic proof that the decision is lawful, correct, prudent, enforceable, funded, executable, or factually justified.

# 5. Roles

TAIP-0002 distinguishes roles because the component creating a record, the identity standing behind the response, the principal represented, and the endpoint receiving the submission may differ.

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acceptance Producer | Component that constructs the Acceptance Record |
| Issuer / Responder | Protocol Identity taking responsibility for the response |
| Principal | Protocol Identity on whose behalf the response is issued |
| Receiving Endpoint | Service or component that received the submitted Intent |
| Target Intent Issuer | Issuer of the referenced Intent Record |
| Intended Actor | Participant expected or permitted to act on the Intent |
| Decision Authority | Human, organization, Agent, or governed role supplying decision Authority |
| Signer | Key-controlling role producing a Signature or proof |
| Resolver | Component resolving identities, keys, Authority, Policy, schemas, and references |
| State Evaluator | Component reconstructing effective Acceptance state from records and transitions |
| Verifier | Role evaluating Acceptance evidence under an explicit Verification Context |

One Protocol Identity may occupy several roles. Role coincidence must remain explicit where it affects Authority, conflicts of interest, independence, or interpretation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-015 — Issuer presence.** Every Acceptance Record MUST identify exactly one logical issuer responsible for the bounded response.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-016 — Principal presence.** Every Acceptance Record MUST identify exactly one logical principal, even when the principal and issuer are the same Protocol Identity.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-017 — Producer visibility.** The Acceptance Producer MUST be identified when it differs from the issuer or when producer identity is required by the applicable Trust Profile.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-018 — Endpoint visibility.** The Receiving Endpoint MUST be identified when its identity is material to submission, routing, acceptance rules, or later Verification.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-019 — Role separation.** Producer, issuer, principal, endpoint, Decision Authority, signer, executor, Registry, and verifier MUST NOT be presumed identical merely because one Protocol Identity appears in several fields.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-020 — Signer role.** Every Signature or equivalent proof MUST identify the role in which its signer acts and the protected statement for which that role is asserted.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-021 — Multi-party boundary.** Acceptance by one required participant MUST NOT be interpreted as Acceptance by another participant or as completion of a threshold rule unless the applicable acceptance rules explicitly establish that conclusion.

# 6. Acceptance Decision Classes

The logical property `decision` identifies the responder's top-level response.

Initial values are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `accepted` | The responder accepts the identified scope without unresolved activation conditions under the recorded rules |
| `conditionally_accepted` | The responder accepts the identified scope only if explicit protected conditions are satisfied |
| `rejected` | The responder declines the identified scope under the recorded rules |
| `deferred` | The responder records that no final acceptance decision has yet been made |

These values describe the responder's protected statement. They do not determine legal effect.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-022 — Governed decision.** `decision` MUST use a value defined by this specification or an incorporated governed Registry or critical extension.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-023 — Accepted semantics.** `accepted` MUST mean the exact target and scope were accepted under the stated rules and MUST NOT silently include unreferenced amendments or unresolved activation conditions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-024 — Conditional semantics.** `conditionally_accepted` MUST identify every protected condition material to activation, reliance, Authorization, or execution.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-025 — Rejection semantics.** `rejected` MUST mean that no accepted state is established for the identified scope by that record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-026 — Deferral semantics.** `deferred` MUST mean that no final Acceptance or rejection is established and MUST NOT be interpreted as temporary Acceptance.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-027 — No success inference.** A syntactically successful response, HTTP success code, queue acknowledgment, or stored record MUST NOT be mapped to `accepted` without the accountable semantic decision required by this specification.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-028 — Unknown decision.** An unknown critical decision value MUST produce an Unsupported result and MUST NOT be coerced to the nearest known value.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-031 — No implicit security fields.** Transport headers, database columns, session state, URLs, or SDK defaults MUST NOT silently supply a mandatory logical property unless an incorporated binding explicitly protects and maps that property.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-032 — Conditional requirements.** A decision class, action schema, Trust Profile, or critical extension MAY add mandatory properties but MUST NOT weaken Core TAIP-0002 requirements.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-033 — Unknown properties.** Unknown properties outside a governed extension location MUST produce an explicit validation result according to the binding and MUST NOT silently alter the protected semantic object.

# 8. Object Type, Version, Context, and Identifier

For this version:

```text
object_type    = taip.acceptance
object_version = 0.1.0
```

`taip_context` identifies every TAIP artifact version material to interpretation. At minimum it contains the versions of `TAIP-0000`, `TAIP-0001`, and `TAIP-0002`, plus incorporated common-envelope, schema, canonicalization, Registry, profile, and binding versions when applicable.

`acceptance_id` is the stable identifier of one finalized Acceptance Record.

```text
Acceptance ID
≠ Acceptance Digest
≠ Intent ID
≠ Workflow ID
≠ Idempotency Key
≠ Submission Receipt ID
≠ Commitment Receipt ID
≠ Storage Locator
```

The initial logical identifier syntax is URI-form under a governed namespace. A binding or profile may define permitted schemes.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-034 — Fixed type.** An Acceptance Record conforming to this specification MUST identify `object_type` as `taip.acceptance`.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-035 — Version binding.** `object_version` and interpretation-critical `taip_context` values MUST be included in the cryptographically protected scope.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-036 — Exact interpretation.** A verifier MUST evaluate an Acceptance Record under the normative versions identified by the protected object and MUST NOT silently substitute current or `latest` rules.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-037 — Unsupported version.** An implementation that cannot interpret a required protected version MUST return Unsupported for affected conclusions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-038 — Version immutability.** The identifier `0.1.0` MUST NOT be reassigned to incompatible Acceptance Record semantics after publication.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-039 — Stable identity.** Every finalized Acceptance Record MUST have exactly one `acceptance_id` assigned no later than finalization and Signature creation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-040 — Namespace rules.** The namespace governing `acceptance_id` MUST define generation responsibility, uniqueness scope, comparison, normalization, persistence, and collision behavior.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-041 — No content ambiguity.** The same `acceptance_id` MUST NOT identify different finalized protected content.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-042 — Duplicate handling.** Re-receipt of the same `acceptance_id` with the same canonical digest MAY be processed idempotently as a duplicate.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-043 — Collision handling.** The same `acceptance_id` with different canonical content or digest MUST be reported as a conflict and MUST NOT be resolved by overwrite or last-write-wins behavior.

# 9. Target Intent Binding

`target_intent` identifies the exact Intent Record to which the responder replies.

It contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `intent_id` | 1 | Stable identifier from TAIP-0001 |
| `object_type` | 1 | Expected `taip.intent` type |
| `object_version` | 1 | Target Intent semantic version |
| `digest` | 1 | Digest of the target's Canonical Representation |
| `digest_algorithm` | 1 | Governed digest algorithm identifier |
| `representation` | 1 | Canonicalization or representation identifier used for the digest |
| `availability` | 1 | `embedded`, `attached`, `resolvable`, `withheld`, or `unavailable` |
| `locator` | 0..n | Optional retrieval locations not used as integrity proof |

**TAIP-0002-REQ-044 — Strong target reference.** Every Acceptance Record MUST bind both the `intent_id` and canonical digest of the target Intent Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-045 — Target type.** The target MUST resolve or declare as `taip.intent` under a supported TAIP-0001 version.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-046 — Digest context.** The protected target reference MUST identify the digest algorithm and Canonical Representation required to reproduce the target digest.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-047 — No locator substitution.** A mutable locator, database key, message ID, or business correlation value MUST NOT substitute for the strong target reference.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-048 — Target availability.** Unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, or unsupported target content MUST remain explicit and MUST limit semantic and completeness conclusions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-049 — Target validity separation.** A structurally valid Acceptance Record MAY reference an invalid or unsupported Intent Record, but the resulting target and Acceptance conclusions MUST report that limitation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-050 — Intent immutability.** Creating an Acceptance Record MUST NOT mutate, annotate in place, or reinterpret the finalized target Intent Record.

# 10. Decision Scope and Acceptance Rules

`decision_scope` states whether the response applies to the entire target Intent or to explicit components of a composite action.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `scope_type` | 1 | `entire_intent` or `components` |
| `component_refs` | 0..n | Integrity-bound identifiers of covered components |
| `excluded_component_refs` | 0..n | Explicitly excluded components |
| `scope_digest` | 0..1 | Optional governed digest of a normalized component set |

`acceptance_rules` identifies the rule set under which the responder made the decision.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `referenced`, `embedded`, `profile_defined`, `not_asserted`, or `unavailable` |
| `rule_refs` | 0..n | Versioned, typed, integrity-aware rule references |
| `evaluation_refs` | 0..n | Evidence supporting evaluation of the rules |
| `required_responders` | 0..n | Identities, roles, or threshold groups required for collective Acceptance |
| `aggregation_rule` | 0..1 | Governed rule for combining multiple responses |
| `unresolved_rules` | 0..n | Rules that could not be evaluated |

**TAIP-0002-REQ-051 — Explicit scope.** Every Acceptance Record MUST explicitly state whether it covers the entire Intent or identified components.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-052 — Component identity.** A component-scoped response MUST use stable, governed, integrity-bound component references defined by the target Intent's action schema.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-053 — No silent partial Acceptance.** A responder MUST NOT represent partial Acceptance as whole-intent `accepted`.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-054 — Partial response.** A response covering only some components MUST identify every included and excluded component and MUST use `conditionally_accepted`, `rejected`, or `deferred` according to governed aggregation semantics.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-055 — Rule status.** Every Acceptance Record MUST state one governed `acceptance_rules.status` value.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-056 — Historical rules.** Referenced acceptance rules MUST bind to the version and effective boundary applied to the decision and MUST NOT rely solely on mutable current rules.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-057 — Rule evaluation separation.** Presence of a rule set or evaluation reference MUST NOT be treated as proof that the rule was correctly evaluated.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-058 — Collective Acceptance.** When multiple responses are required, each response and the aggregation rule MUST remain independently attributable and verifiable.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-059 — Unresolved rule.** An unavailable, unsupported, conflicted, or unevaluated mandatory acceptance rule MUST prevent an unconditional `accepted` conclusion unless an explicit governed override applies.

# 11. Conditions, Deferral, and Reasons

`conditions` contains the protected conditions attached to `conditionally_accepted`. Each condition contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `condition_id` | 1 | Stable identifier within the Acceptance Record |
| `condition_type` | 1 | Governed semantic class |
| `operator` | 1 | Governed evaluation operation |
| `value` | 1 | Typed value or integrity-bound reference |
| `subject` | 0..1 | Action field, participant, state, or evidence target |
| `deadline` | 0..1 | Latest valid satisfaction boundary |
| `satisfaction_evidence` | 0..n | Evidence type or reference required to prove satisfaction |
| `failure_effect` | 1 | Governed result when the condition is not satisfied |

`reason` may contain a governed `code`, typed details, and an optional `human_summary`. Reasons may explain rejection, deferral, conditional Acceptance, withdrawal, or supersession.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-060 — Condition identity.** Each condition MUST have a unique `condition_id` within the Acceptance Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-061 — Governed evaluation.** A condition's type, operator, value type, comparison behavior, evidence requirement, and failure effect MUST be defined by an incorporated normative source.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-062 — Conditional consistency.** `conditionally_accepted` MUST contain at least one material condition, and `accepted` MUST NOT contain an unresolved activation condition.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-063 — Satisfaction evidence.** A claim that a condition was satisfied MUST identify the required evidence and evaluation context; elapsed time or operational state alone MUST NOT silently satisfy it.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-064 — Deferral detail.** A `deferred` response SHOULD identify the cause, expected next event, responsible role, and review or expiry boundary when known.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-065 — Reason typing.** A reason material to automated interpretation MUST use governed structured semantics and MUST NOT depend solely on free-form text.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-066 — Sensitive reason.** A reason MAY be withheld, redacted, encrypted, or selectively disclosed, but its availability status and any resulting Verification limitation MUST remain explicit.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-067 — No amendment by reason.** A reason or human summary MUST NOT silently amend the target Intent, decision scope, conditions, or applicable rules.

# 14. Temporal Context and Validity

`temporal_context` identifies the time meanings applicable to the Acceptance Record.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `decision_time` | 1 | Issuer-claimed time of the response decision |
| `record_time` | 1 | Issuer-claimed time of finalization |
| `not_before` | 0..1 | Earliest boundary at which the response may be relied upon |
| `expires_at` | 0..1 | Latest boundary for prospective reliance |
| `review_at` | 0..1 | Expected reevaluation time, especially for deferral |
| `time_source` | 1 | Governed description or reference for time origin |
| `precision` | 1 | Declared time precision |
| `uncertainty` | 0..1 | Declared uncertainty bound |
| `sequence` | 0..1 | Governed responder-local sequence or state counter |

These times remain distinct from Intent event time, Submission Time, Signature Time, Commitment Time, execution time, settlement time, and Verification Time.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-079 — Required times.** Every Acceptance Record MUST contain `decision_time`, `record_time`, `time_source`, and `precision`.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-080 — Time syntax.** Instants and intervals MUST use the exact syntax, timezone, precision, leap-second behavior, and comparison rules defined by the incorporated binding.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-081 — Time ordering.** A verifier MUST evaluate required temporal relationships and MUST report impossible or policy-invalid orderings.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-082 — Intent validity.** A responder or verifier MUST evaluate whether the target Intent was within its stated validity boundary at the relevant Acceptance decision boundary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-083 — Expiry semantics.** `expires_at` limits prospective reliance on the Acceptance statement and MUST NOT erase that the response occurred.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-084 — Deferral expiry.** A deferred response whose protected review or expiry boundary has passed MUST NOT be represented as an active pending decision without additional accountable state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-085 — Clock limitation.** Producer-asserted time MUST NOT be represented as externally proven time without supporting Commitment, Witness, Checkpoint, anchor, or trusted-time evidence.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-086 — Historical evaluation.** Later Verification MUST evaluate the key, Authority, Policy, Intent, and acceptance-rule state applicable at the decision boundary rather than substituting current state.

# 15. Submission and Receipt Context

`submission_context` records the bounded evidence available about delivery of the target Intent to the responder.

It may include:

- sender and receiving endpoint identities;
- submission identifier and attempt number;
- claimed Submission Time and receipt time;
- transport or queue metadata;
- a Submission Receipt or transport acknowledgment reference;
- batch identity and per-item status;
- delivery-integrity or channel-binding evidence;
- missing, unavailable, or disputed receipt evidence.

```text
Transport Delivered
≠ Submission Acknowledged
≠ Semantically Accepted
≠ Historically Committed
```

**TAIP-0002-REQ-087 — Explicit submission status.** Every Acceptance Record MUST state whether submission evidence is `referenced`, `embedded`, `locally_observed`, `not_available`, or `not_applicable`.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-088 — Receipt separation.** A transport acknowledgment, queue receipt, storage confirmation, or successful API response MUST NOT by itself establish semantic Acceptance.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-089 — Channel binding.** Channel identity or authenticated transport MAY support provenance but MUST NOT replace the protected issuer proof on the Acceptance Record when such proof is required.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-090 — Batch isolation.** Acceptance of a batch or envelope MUST NOT imply Acceptance of every contained Intent unless per-item coverage and results are explicit.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-091 — Attempt visibility.** Multiple delivery attempts MUST preserve attempt identity and MUST NOT create duplicate semantic Acceptance or erase conflicting responses.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-092 — Missing receipt.** Absence of receipt evidence MUST remain distinct from evidence that the Intent was not received.

# 16. Relationships, Workflow, Retry, and Idempotency

`relationships` is a set of typed references connecting the Acceptance Record to related accountability state.

Initial relationship types include:

| Relationship | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `responds_to` | Strong target relationship to the Intent Record |
| `caused_by` | Causal evidence contributing to the response |
| `evaluated_under` | Rules, Policy, Authority, or profile evidence |
| `previous_response` | Earlier response in the same responder/principal/scope lineage |
| `supersedes` | Earlier Acceptance replaced by this response |
| `counter_intent` | New Intent proposing modified terms |
| `condition_evidence` | Evidence relevant to a protected condition |
| `workflow` | Shared workflow correlation without target semantics |
| `retry_of` | Earlier processing attempt with the same semantic request |
| `related_execution` | Later execution evidence, without implying success |

`idempotency_key` MAY be carried in a governed relationship or submission context. It is not an object identifier.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-093 — Typed relationships.** Every accountability-critical relationship MUST use governed semantics and identify the expected target type, identity, and required integrity strength.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-094 — Strong response relationship.** `responds_to`, `previous_response`, and `supersedes` references MUST be integrity-bound when substitution could change effective Acceptance state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-095 — Correlation boundary.** A workflow ID, message ID, transaction ID, or idempotency key MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that two Protocol Objects have the same content or lifecycle state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-096 — Retry semantics.** A retry MUST identify whether it reprocesses the same protected response, requests a new response, or responds to a replacement Intent.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-097 — Idempotent duplicate.** Identical canonical Acceptance content under the same `acceptance_id` MAY be deduplicated without creating new semantic state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-098 — Conflicting duplicate.** Different protected content under the same identifier or idempotency claim MUST remain visible as a conflict.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-099 — Counterproposal boundary.** Modified action terms, scope, amount, destination, or other Intent semantics MUST be expressed through a new Intent Record and MUST NOT be hidden in an Acceptance reason or untyped condition.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-100 — Cycle control.** Resolvers and verifiers MUST enforce governed cycle, depth, size, and retrieval limits for relationship graphs.

`trust_context` states whether the response claims evaluation under a Trust Profile.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `specified`, `not_specified`, `unavailable`, or `unsupported` |
| `profile_id` | 0..1 | Stable Trust Profile identifier |
| `profile_version` | 0..1 | Exact profile version |
| `profile_digest` | 0..1 | Integrity commitment where required |
| `evaluation_refs` | 0..n | Evidence or reports relevant to profile evaluation |

**TAIP-0002-REQ-101 — Explicit profile status.** Every Acceptance Record MUST state one governed Trust Profile status.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-102 — Exact profile.** A specified profile MUST bind its stable identifier and exact version, plus an integrity commitment when required by that profile.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-103 — Profile claim boundary.** Naming a Trust Profile MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the response, deployment, or complete workflow satisfied it.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-104 — Profile result.** Profile achievement MUST be determined from the required evidence and MUST produce explicit limitations for missing, unsupported, or unevaluated controls.

# 18. Confidentiality and Data Minimization

`confidentiality` identifies the classification and disclosure behavior of Acceptance evidence.

It may define:

- classification;
- permitted recipient roles;
- encrypted or selectively disclosed fields;
- commitment-only fields;
- retention and deletion policy references;
- redaction semantics;
- disclosure and audit events;
- handling of sensitive rejection or fraud indicators.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-105 — Required classification.** Every Acceptance Record MUST include a governed confidentiality classification, including an explicit public or unclassified value when appropriate.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-106 — Data minimization.** A producer MUST minimize personal, financial, commercial, security, and model-internal data to what is necessary for the intended accountability claim and profile.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-107 — No secret leakage.** Secrets, raw credentials, private keys, bearer tokens, unnecessary account data, or unrestricted internal reasoning MUST NOT be included in Acceptance evidence.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-108 — Protected omission.** Withholding, encryption, redaction, or selective disclosure of a mandatory field MUST preserve an explicit protected indication of its status and resulting Verification dependency.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-109 — Metadata privacy.** Implementations MUST consider that responder identity, rejection code, timing, Policy references, workflow links, and condition details may reveal sensitive activity even when action parameters are encrypted.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-110 — Completeness boundary.** Privacy protection MUST NOT cause unavailable evidence to be silently treated as satisfied, absent, or not applicable.

# 19. Extensions

`extensions` provides the governed location for additional semantics.

Each extension must identify:

- a collision-resistant extension identifier;
- extension version;
- criticality;
- schema or interpretation reference;
- protected extension value;
- compatibility and failure behavior.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-111 — Governed namespace.** An extension identifier MUST be governed and collision-resistant.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-112 — Protected criticality.** Extension identity, version, criticality, and value MUST be included in the same protected semantic scope as the Acceptance Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-113 — Unknown critical extension.** A verifier that does not support a critical extension MUST return Unsupported for affected conclusions and MUST NOT ignore the extension.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-114 — Unknown non-critical extension.** A non-critical extension MAY be ignored semantically only when the governing extension rules say that doing so preserves Core meaning; its protected bytes MUST still participate in digest and Signature processing.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-115 — No Core redefinition.** An extension MUST NOT redefine Core fields, weaken target binding, downgrade protected conditions, collapse lifecycle states, or turn missing evidence into success.

# 20. Canonical Representation and Digest

Before identifier validation, digesting, signing, or Commitment, the protected logical object is converted into a deterministic Canonical Representation under the declared method.

Canonicalization must define at least:

- property ordering;
- string encoding and normalization;
- number and exact-decimal representation;
- time representation;
- absent, null, and empty values;
- array ordering or set normalization;
- identifier comparison and normalization;
- extension handling;
- duplicate-property rejection;
- external or encrypted value commitments;
- Signature-container exclusion or placeholder rules.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-116 — Declared method.** Every Acceptance Record MUST identify the Canonical Representation method and exact version used for its protected digest and proofs.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-117 — Determinism.** Conforming implementations given the same protected logical object and method version MUST produce identical canonical bytes.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-118 — Protected scope.** The canonical protected scope MUST include every interpretation-critical field, including target binding, decision, scope, rules, conditions, issuer, principal, Authority, Policy, time, relationships, confidentiality, extensions, and version context.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-119 — Duplicate fields.** A binding MUST reject duplicate or ambiguous property representations before canonicalization.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-120 — Digest algorithm.** Every digest MUST identify a governed algorithm and parameters; an unsupported or prohibited algorithm MUST produce an explicit result.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-121 — Transport independence.** Digest and Signature verification MUST operate on the governed Canonical Representation and MUST NOT assume received transport bytes are canonical.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-122 — Cross-implementation vectors.** Stable TAIP-0002 bindings MUST publish positive and negative canonicalization, digest, and parser-differential test vectors.

# 21. Signatures and Proofs

`proofs` attributes the response statement to the issuer or other governed role.

Each proof contains or integrity-binds:

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `proof_type` | 1 | Governed Signature or proof class |
| `signer` | 1 | Protocol Identity controlling the proof |
| `role` | 1 | Role asserted for this proof |
| `key_id` | 1 | Key identifier |
| `key_purpose` | 1 | Governed Key Purpose |
| `algorithm` | 1 | Algorithm and parameter identifier |
| `target_digest` | 1 | Digest of the protected Acceptance statement |
| `signature_time` | 0..1 | Signer-claimed Signature Time |
| `value` | 1 | Proof value or integrity-bound reference |

**TAIP-0002-REQ-123 — Issuer attribution.** Every Acceptance Record MUST contain at least one valid proof attributable to the issuer under a governed Key Purpose.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-124 — Exact proof target.** A proof MUST bind the exact canonical digest of the finalized Acceptance Record and MUST NOT rely solely on a detached unprotected identifier.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-125 — Historical Key State.** Verification MUST evaluate key validity, status, purpose, compromise, rotation, and revocation at the relevant Signature or decision boundary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-126 — Signature/Authority separation.** A valid Signature proves cryptographic attribution under the evaluated key state; it MUST NOT by itself establish Authority to accept or reject the Intent.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-127 — Multi-proof semantics.** Multiple proofs MUST identify their distinct roles, thresholds, ordering, and aggregation semantics and MUST NOT be interpreted solely as a count.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-128 — Proof failure.** Missing, malformed, unsupported, invalid, out-of-purpose, revoked, or historically indeterminate proof state MUST remain explicit in Verification results.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-129 — Algorithm agility.** Proof processing MUST use governed algorithms, parameters, deprecation state, and migration rules without silent downgrade.

# 22. Finalization and Lifecycle

Before finalization, an Acceptance draft may be revised. Finalization fixes the protected logical content used for digesting and signing.

```text
Draft
  │ validate for creation
  ▼
Finalized
  │ sign
  ▼
Signed
  │ submit
  ▼
Submitted
  │ receiver or Registry acknowledges
  ▼
Accepted for Processing
  │ commitment service acts
  ▼
Committed
```

The `Accepted for Processing` state above concerns the Acceptance Record's own submission lifecycle. It is not the same semantic statement as the `decision` inside the record. Implementations should label these concepts distinctly in APIs and user interfaces.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-130 — Finalization boundary.** Finalization MUST establish the complete protected Acceptance content before issuer proof generation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-131 — Post-finalization change.** Any change to protected content after finalization MUST create a new object or an explicitly related replacement and MUST NOT retain the old digest or proofs.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-132 — Lifecycle distinction.** Draft, finalized, signed, submitted, accepted for processing, committed, witnessed, checkpointed, anchored, preserved, and verified states MUST remain distinct.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-133 — Decision/lifecycle distinction.** The Acceptance Record's internal `decision` MUST NOT be confused with the later acceptance of that record by a Registry, Commitment Service, or transport endpoint.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-134 — Submission boundary.** A submitted or queued Acceptance Record MUST NOT be represented as committed merely because delivery was attempted or acknowledged.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-135 — Commitment boundary.** Commitment of an Acceptance Record proves entry into defined protected history and MUST NOT by itself prove Authority, Policy compliance, execution, or truth.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-136 — Immutable history.** Finalized and especially committed Acceptance content MUST NOT be silently modified; correction and changed decisions require additional accountable state.

# 23. Effective Acceptance State

Effective Acceptance state is reconstructed for a specific tuple:

```text
(target Intent, responder, principal, decision scope, acceptance rules)
```

Possible evaluated states include:

- `no_response`;
- `deferred`;
- `rejected`;
- `conditionally_accepted`;
- `accepted`;
- `withdrawn`;
- `superseded`;
- `expired`;
- `conflicted`;
- `indeterminate`.

`no_response` is an absence-of-evidence result within the evaluated material. It is not evidence that the responder rejected or did not receive the Intent.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-137 — State tuple.** An effective-state claim MUST identify the target Intent, responder, principal, scope, rules, and evaluation boundary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-138 — Evidence-based state.** Except for an explicitly scoped `no_response` result, an effective state MUST be supported by validated Acceptance Records and applicable transition evidence.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-139 — No latest-write shortcut.** A state evaluator MUST NOT choose an effective response solely by database update time, retrieval order, or highest untrusted timestamp.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-140 — Conflict visibility.** Concurrent, non-ordered, or incompatible responses that cannot be resolved under protected rules MUST produce `conflicted` or `indeterminate` rather than arbitrary selection.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-141 — Condition state.** `conditionally_accepted` MUST NOT become `accepted` without the evidence and transition or aggregation behavior required by its protected conditions and applicable rules.

# 24. Acceptance Transition Record

An Acceptance Transition Record adds accountable state about an earlier Acceptance Record.

For this version:

```text
object_type    = taip.acceptance.transition
object_version = 0.1.0
```

Initial transition types are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `withdraw` | Issuer states that prospective reliance on the earlier response is withdrawn |
| `supersede` | A new Acceptance Record replaces the earlier response for a defined scope |
| `expire` | Issuer records expiry under identified rules or evidence |
| `correct` | Issuer corrects non-decision information through additional protected state |
| `annotate` | Issuer adds bounded explanatory or evidentiary context without changing decision semantics |

The logical model contains:

| Property | Cardinality | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| `object_type` | 1 | Fixed transition type |
| `object_version` | 1 | Transition semantic version |
| `taip_context` | 1 | Applicable TAIP versions |
| `transition_id` | 1 | Stable transition identifier |
| `transition_type` | 1 | Governed transition class |
| `target_acceptance` | 1 | Strong reference to the earlier Acceptance Record |
| `replacement_acceptance` | 0..1 | Strong reference required for supersession |
| `issuer` | 1 | Identity responsible for the transition |
| `principal` | 1 | Identity on whose behalf the transition is issued |
| `authority_context` | 1 | Authority status and evidence |
| `policy_context` | 1 | Policy status and evidence |
| `effective_time` | 1 | Claimed effective boundary |
| `reason` | 0..1 | Governed reason |
| `relationships` | 1 | Related evidence, possibly empty |
| `extensions` | 1 | Governed extensions, possibly empty |
| `canonicalization` | 1 | Canonical Representation method |
| `proofs` | 1..n | Issuer-attribution proofs |

**TAIP-0002-REQ-142 — Append-only transition.** A transition MUST create a new independently identifiable protected object and MUST NOT modify the target Acceptance Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-143 — Strong transition target.** `target_acceptance` MUST bind the target's `acceptance_id`, object type, object version, canonical digest, digest algorithm, and representation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-144 — Transition Authority.** The transition issuer MUST provide Authority and Policy context sufficient to evaluate its right to create the claimed transition.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-145 — Supersession replacement.** `supersede` MUST bind exactly one replacement Acceptance Record and MUST define the affected scope and effective boundary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-146 — Correction boundary.** A `correct` or `annotate` transition MUST NOT silently change the earlier decision, target Intent, responder, principal, or scope; such changes require a replacement Acceptance Record.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-147 — Non-retroactivity.** Withdrawal, supersession, correction, annotation, or expiry MUST NOT erase the historical fact that the earlier response was issued.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-148 — Effect boundary.** A transition's effect on obligations, prior reliance, Authorization, or already initiated execution MUST be determined by applicable Policy, Authority, ordering evidence, and domain rules rather than assumed from the transition type alone.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-149 — Transition conflict.** Conflicting, cyclic, unauthorized, or non-orderable transitions MUST remain visible and MUST produce a bounded state result.

# 25. Boundary with Authorization, Execution, and Commitment

The following distinctions are normative:

```text
Intent Submitted
≠ Intent Received
≠ Intent Accepted
≠ Action Authorized
≠ Execution Requested
≠ Execution Accepted
≠ Action Executed
≠ Result or Settlement
≠ Evidence Committed
```

An execution system may use different domain terms such as accepted, authorized, pending, completed, settled, or final. TAIP mappings must identify their exact evidence meaning and must not collapse them into TAIP Acceptance.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-150 — Authorization boundary.** Acceptance MUST NOT by itself establish that the responder, principal, Intended Actor, or execution system was Authorized to perform the action.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-151 — Execution boundary.** Acceptance MUST NOT by itself establish that an execution request was sent, received, accepted by an execution system, processed, completed, or settled.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-152 — Commitment boundary.** Acceptance MUST NOT by itself establish that the Intent, Acceptance Record, or related evidence entered a Hash Chain or other protected historical state.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-153 — Intent cancellation race.** Acceptance that races with cancellation, withdrawal, expiry, or supersession of the target Intent MUST be evaluated using protected time, ordering, Authority, Policy, and transition evidence.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-154 — Domain mapping.** A mapping from an external system's status to a TAIP decision or lifecycle state MUST be explicit, versioned, testable, and bounded to the evidence actually supplied by that system.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-155 — No legal inference.** Acceptance MUST NOT be represented as contract formation, legal consent, regulatory approval, or enforceability unless a separate governed profile and applicable law support that specific claim.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-156 — UI wording.** User interfaces and APIs MUST distinguish receipt, semantic Acceptance, Authorization, execution, result, and Commitment in labels and status values.

# 26. Validation Procedure

A TAIP-0002 validator evaluates the Acceptance Record in ordered dimensions. Later success does not erase an earlier failure.

## 26.1 Parsing and Binding

Confirm the declared binding, media type, encoding, duplicate-property rules, size limits, and object boundaries.

## 26.2 Structural Validation

Confirm required properties, cardinalities, types, decision-specific fields, and extension placement.

## 26.3 Target Validation

Resolve or obtain the Intent Record, reproduce its canonical digest, compare identifiers and versions, and report unavailable or unsupported target evidence.

## 26.4 Semantic Validation

Evaluate decision value, scope, rules, conditions, reasons, time semantics, and relationship compatibility.

## 26.5 Canonical and Cryptographic Validation

Reproduce canonical bytes and digest, validate proofs, algorithms, Key Purpose, and Historical Key State.

## 26.6 Authority and Policy Validation

Evaluate responder Authority, delegation, approvals, Policy, exceptions, effective boundaries, and conflicts.

## 26.7 Lifecycle and Transition Validation

Evaluate finalization, submission, Commitment evidence, duplicate state, prior responses, transitions, target Intent transitions, and ordering.

## 26.8 Profile and Completeness Validation

Evaluate all controls and dependencies required by the selected Trust Profile and Verification claim.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-157 — Layered validation.** Validation MUST preserve separate results for parsing, structure, target, semantics, canonicalization, cryptography, identity, Authority, Policy, time, lifecycle, profile, and completeness.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-158 — Validation order.** An implementation MUST NOT make a semantic or cryptographic success claim when prerequisite parsing or canonicalization failed.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-159 — Exact target verification.** A verifier claiming target validity MUST reproduce and compare the target Intent digest under the declared Canonical Representation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-160 — Historical dependencies.** Validation MUST use the key, Authority, Policy, acceptance-rule, profile, and transition state applicable at the evaluated boundary.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-161 — Unknown critical semantics.** Unsupported critical decisions, conditions, relationships, extensions, algorithms, or versions MUST produce Unsupported for affected conclusions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-162 — Resource controls.** Parsers and resolvers MUST enforce governed input, nesting, graph-depth, reference-count, retrieval-size, and processing-time limits.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-163 — No silent repair.** A validator MUST NOT silently normalize a material ambiguity, invent a missing field, select a conflicting response, or repair protected content.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-164 — Reproducible inputs.** A validation result MUST identify the exact object digests, dependency versions, Verification Time, and evaluation configuration used.

# 27. Acceptance Verification Result

A verifier emits dimensioned results rather than one overloaded boolean.

Initial result values are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Pass` | Available evidence satisfies the evaluated rule |
| `Fail` | Available evidence contradicts or violates the evaluated rule |
| `Indeterminate` | Evidence is insufficient, unavailable, conflicted, or temporally non-orderable |
| `Unsupported` | Required semantics or algorithms are not supported |
| `NotApplicable` | The rule does not apply under the explicit context |

A Verification result should include at least:

- result identifier and Verification Time;
- verifier identity and implementation version where attributable;
- Acceptance and target Intent identifiers and digests;
- Verification Context and requested claims;
- normative artifact, profile, Registry, and algorithm versions;
- dimensioned findings;
- effective Acceptance state if evaluated;
- missing, unavailable, redacted, encrypted, conflicted, or unsupported dependencies;
- warnings, limits, and non-protocol assumptions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-165 — Dimensioned result.** A verifier MUST report findings by validation dimension and MUST NOT collapse materially different failures into one generic invalid status.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-166 — Bounded conclusion.** A Verification conclusion MUST state exactly whether it concerns object integrity, issuer attribution, target binding, Authority, Policy, temporal validity, effective state, profile satisfaction, or another defined claim.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-167 — Missing evidence.** Missing, unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, conflicted, expired, revoked, or unsupported evidence MUST remain visible in the result.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-168 — No current-state substitution.** A verifier MUST NOT replace unavailable Historical State with current key, Authority, Policy, rule, profile, or lifecycle state without explicit limitation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-169 — Deterministic composition.** Result precedence and composition MUST be defined so independent conforming verifiers given the same inputs and context reach compatible protocol conclusions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-170 — Verification boundary.** A valid Verification Report is evidence of an evaluation under a defined context; it is not automatic proof of business truth, legal effect, or evidence Completeness beyond that context.

# 28. Portability, Packaging, and Preservation

Acceptance evidence must remain independently interpretable outside the producer's application.

A portable package may contain:

- the Acceptance Record and relevant transitions;
- the target Intent Record and its transitions;
- Canonical Representation and schema material;
- identity, key, Authority, Policy, and Historical State evidence;
- acceptance rules and evaluation evidence;
- submission and receipt evidence;
- Commitment, Witness, Checkpoint, anchor, and Preservation evidence;
- Trust Profile and Registry dependencies;
- manifests describing included, external, unavailable, or withheld material.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-171 — Portable interpretation.** A conforming export MUST preserve the protected Acceptance semantics without requiring access to the original producer's private database or user interface.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-172 — Dependency manifest.** A package MUST identify every dependency required for the claimed Verification result and its availability, integrity, version, and disclosure status.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-173 — Historical material.** Long-term preservation MUST retain or integrity-bind the target Intent, response, transitions, keys, Authority, Policy, rules, profile, and specification versions required for future interpretation.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-174 — Package boundary.** Valid package structure or package integrity MUST NOT be interpreted as Acceptance validity, effective state, evidence Completeness, or Trust Profile achievement.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-175 — Unavailable dependency.** A preserved package MUST identify dependencies that are external, unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, unsupported, or not evaluated.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-176 — Migration relationship.** A migrated Acceptance representation MUST bind source and target digests, transformation method, tool and version, authority, time, validation result, and information loss.

# 29. Conformance

TAIP-0002 conformance is role-specific.

## 29.1 Producer Conformance

A conforming producer creates complete logical objects, strongly binds the target Intent, assigns stable IDs, applies required schemas and rules, finalizes protected content, and emits issuer proofs.

## 29.2 Parser Conformance

A conforming parser recognizes the binding and exposes every protected logical property without silent coercion or loss.

## 29.3 Validator Conformance

A conforming validator evaluates structural, target, semantic, canonicalization, cryptographic, identity, Authority, Policy, temporal, relationship, and lifecycle rules within its declared capability scope.

## 29.4 State Evaluator Conformance

A conforming state evaluator reconstructs effective Acceptance state using validated records, transitions, rules, and ordering evidence without last-write-wins shortcuts.

## 29.5 Transition Producer Conformance

A conforming transition producer creates append-only, strongly target-bound, authorized transition evidence.

## 29.6 Verifier Conformance

A conforming verifier evaluates available evidence under an explicit context and emits dimensioned bounded results.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-177 — Declared role.** Every TAIP-0002 conformance claim MUST identify the implemented role and exact specification, schema, canonicalization, Registry, profile, binding, and test-suite versions.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-178 — Positive tests.** Conformance tests MUST cover valid records for every supported decision, whole and component scope, rule status, Authority status, Policy status, condition form, validity form, extension mode, transition type, and proof suite.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-179 — Negative tests.** Conformance tests MUST cover missing fields, weak target references, ID collisions, unknown critical semantics, invalid decisions, partial-scope ambiguity, unsatisfied conditions, invalid canonicalization, invalid proofs, Authority failure, Policy failure, time errors, conflicts, and resource limits.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-180 — Boundary tests.** Tests MUST distinguish transport receipt, semantic Acceptance, Authorization, execution, result, Commitment, current state, and business truth.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-181 — Transition tests.** Tests MUST cover valid, unauthorized, conflicting, cyclic, late, and non-orderable transitions plus races with Intent cancellation and execution.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-182 — Cross-implementation tests.** Canonicalization, digest, Signature input, target binding, identifier comparison, temporal evaluation, state reconstruction, and result composition MUST be tested across independent implementations before stable release.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-183 — Test traceability.** Every normative `TAIP-0002-REQ-*` rule MUST map to one or more test assertions or an explicit justified non-testable disposition.

**TAIP-0002-REQ-184 — Scoped report.** Passing a parser or producer subset MUST NOT be represented as full TAIP-0002 verifier or deployment conformance, and reports MUST identify passed, failed, skipped, unsupported, and not-applicable tests.

# 33. Security Considerations

## 33.1 Forged Acceptance

An attacker may create or alter a response to make an Intent appear accepted by another party. Strong target binding, protected responder identity, deterministic canonicalization, Signatures, Historical Key State, and Authority evaluation are required defenses.

## 33.2 Intent Substitution

An attacker may replace the target with another Intent sharing a workflow ID, amount, or display summary. The Acceptance Record must bind the exact `intent_id`, digest, algorithm, version, and representation.

## 33.3 Receipt Confusion

A transport acknowledgment or successful API response may be presented as semantic Acceptance. Protocol status and business decision fields must remain separately named, protected, and verified.

## 33.4 Condition Stripping

An attacker may remove funding, approval, timing, counterparty, or evidence conditions to transform conditional Acceptance into unconditional Acceptance. All conditions and their evaluation semantics must lie inside the protected scope.

## 33.5 Authority Confusion

A valid signer or authenticated endpoint may lack Authority to accept the action. Verification must separately resolve Key Purpose, delegation, scope, limits, Policy, approvals, and revocation at the historical boundary.

## 33.6 Replay and Duplicate Responses

A valid old Acceptance Record may be replayed for a replacement Intent or new attempt. Target binding, validity, responder sequence, idempotency, transition history, and execution history must be evaluated together.

## 33.7 Conflicting Responses

Compromised, partitioned, or independently operating responder instances may issue incompatible responses. Implementations must preserve conflicts and use governed ordering or threshold rules rather than last-write-wins behavior.

## 33.8 Cancellation and Execution Races

Intent cancellation, Acceptance withdrawal, execution, and Commitment may occur concurrently. Correct interpretation requires protected event identities, effective times, Authority, Policy, and externally supported ordering evidence.

## 33.9 Parser Differential

Duplicate fields, Unicode normalization, number formats, time parsing, array ordering, unknown properties, and extension behavior may create cross-implementation disagreement. Stable bindings require deterministic rejection rules and test vectors.

## 33.10 Reason and Oracle Abuse

Detailed rejection reasons may reveal fraud controls, Policy thresholds, account state, or operational capacity. Systems should use governed disclosure classes and avoid turning response APIs into sensitive-state oracles.

## 33.11 Overclaiming

The central semantic attack is presenting valid Acceptance as Authorization, execution, settlement, historical Commitment, legal consent, or business truth. Verifiers and user interfaces must preserve the bounded claim.

# 34. Privacy Considerations

Acceptance evidence may expose proposed financial activity, organizational decisions, approval limits, rejection causes, policy controls, liquidity, fraud signals, participant relationships, timing, and system capacity.

Implementations should minimize exposure through:

- commitments or digests instead of unnecessary plaintext;
- separated encrypted payloads;
- selective disclosure of decision reasons and conditions;
- role-limited access;
- privacy-aware identity and workflow references;
- minimized endpoint and correlation metadata;
- disclosure-specific Dispute Packs;
- protected resolver requests;
- retention appropriate to the claim and Trust Profile.

Metadata may remain sensitive even when action parameters and reason details are encrypted. Decision class, responder, timing, profile, Authority references, transition history, and response latency can reveal commercial or security-sensitive activity.

Privacy restrictions do not authorize silent completeness. A verifier unable to access required information must report the limitation.

# 35. Operational Considerations

Implementations must account for:

- offline and delayed Acceptance creation;
- asynchronous delivery and Commitment;
- duplicate submissions and idempotent response reuse;
- Intent expiry during evaluation;
- cancellation and replacement races;
- conditional Acceptance awaiting later evidence;
- multiple required responders and threshold aggregation;
- partial batch and composite-action decisions;
- resolver, identity, key, Policy, and Registry outages;
- clock skew and uncertain event ordering;
- key rotation between issuance and Verification;
- long-term preservation of withdrawn, expired, or superseded responses;
- user-interface wording that does not overstate lifecycle or business state.

A system may fail closed, fail open, queue, defer, reject, or require manual review according to its Authority, Policy, and Trust Profile. The response actually issued and the assurance actually achieved must remain explicit.

Operational logs may support diagnosis but do not replace the protected Acceptance Record or its lifecycle evidence.

# 39. Summary

TAIP-0002 defines a portable and attributable response to one exact Intent Record.

An Acceptance Record identifies:

- the exact target Intent and protected decision scope;
- the responder, principal, endpoint, and relevant participants;
- the acceptance rules, conditions, deferral state, and reasons;
- Authority, Policy, temporal, submission, and Trust Profile context;
- confidentiality, extension, Canonical Representation, and proof semantics.

An Acceptance Transition Record adds withdrawal, supersession, expiry, correction, or annotation without rewriting the original response.

The central boundary is:

```text
Receipt
≠ Acceptance
≠ Authorization
≠ Execution
≠ Result
≠ Commitment
≠ Legal Effect
```

TAIP-0002 makes the receiver's decision independently inspectable: exactly what was accepted or declined, by whom, on whose behalf, under which rules and conditions, and with what evidence.

The next specification, `TAIP-0003`, defines Execution Evidence for requests, responses, effects, failures, completion, and result claims produced by execution systems and counterparties.
