> **TAIP exists so independently implemented systems can create, exchange, preserve, and verify durable accountability evidence for consequential AI-driven actions without depending on one producer's private logs or proprietary interpretation.**

## Abstract

This document charters the **TrustAgentAI Interoperability Protocol (TAIP)**.

It defines the mission, scope, authority, architectural constraints, document structure, normative language, conformance model, versioning rules, extension policy, security and privacy boundaries, change process, and initial specification roadmap for the TAIP series.

TAIP is the normative interoperability layer derived from the **TrustAgentAI Project Bible**. The Project Bible defines durable architectural intent. TAIP converts that intent into precise, versioned, independently implementable, and testable protocol obligations.

This charter is normative for how TAIP specifications are created, interpreted, versioned, reviewed, published, extended, and claimed. It does not by itself define a complete wire format, production Trust Profile, object schema, cryptographic suite, network binding, or implementation API.

Version `0.1.0` is a Draft. It permits design review and experimental implementation but does not authorize an unqualified claim of stable TAIP conformance.

# 2. Normative Language

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **REQUIRED**, **SHALL**, **SHALL NOT**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, **RECOMMENDED**, **NOT RECOMMENDED**, **MAY**, and **OPTIONAL** in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2119) and [RFC 8174](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8174) when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.

Normative meaning may also be expressed by:

- formal data models;
- state machines;
- algorithms and pseudocode;
- tables explicitly labeled normative;
- Registry constraints;
- schema assertions;
- canonicalization procedures;
- outcome-composition rules;
- testable semantic invariants.

Informative examples, diagrams, rationale, implementation notes, and explanatory text do not independently create conformance obligations.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-004 — Requirement precision.** A normative requirement MUST identify its subject, applicability, obligation or prohibition, and observable result precisely enough for independent implementation and evaluation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-005 — Normative consistency.** Normative prose, tables, algorithms, schemas, Registries, and test assertions that express the same rule MUST NOT define incompatible behavior.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-006 — Requirement identifiers.** Each independently testable TAIP requirement MUST have a stable identifier or an unambiguous mapping to a stable normative rule.

The requirement identifier form established by this charter is:

```text
TAIP-<document-number>-REQ-<sequence>
```

Example:

```text
TAIP-0000-REQ-006
```

Published requirement identifiers MUST NOT be reassigned to incompatible meaning.

# 3. Mission

TAIP's mission is to enable interoperable, durable, independently verifiable accountability evidence for consequential actions performed, authorized, coordinated, or materially influenced by AI systems.

TAIP is designed to let a verifier evaluate bounded questions such as:

- what Accountable Action is being claimed;
- which Protocol Identities and roles participated;
- which keys protected the evidence and for what Key Purpose;
- which Authority and Policy applied;
- which lifecycle states were reached;
- whether evidence entered protected historical state;
- whether qualifying independent parties observed that state;
- whether relevant Checkpoints or External Anchors exist;
- which Historical Key State and normative versions apply;
- whether evidence and Verification Dependencies were preserved;
- whether the available evidence achieved an identified Trust Profile;
- which limitations, conflicts, omissions, or unsupported semantics remain.

TAIP does not require a verifier to trust the Evidence Producer's operational database, proprietary dashboard, private implementation, or retrospective narrative as the sole source of protocol meaning.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-007 — Independent interpretability.** Normative TAIP evidence MUST be interpretable by an independent conforming implementation using the applicable preserved specification set and Verification Dependencies.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-008 — Evidence over assertion.** A TAIP Accountability Claim requiring independent assurance MUST NOT rely solely on an unprotected assertion controlled by the party whose action is being evaluated.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-009 — Bounded conclusions.** TAIP Verification conclusions MUST identify their evaluated claim, evidence scope, applicable rules, assumptions, and limitations.

# 6. Non-Goals

TAIP is not:

- an AI model or Agent framework;
- a payment rail, ledger, bank, wallet, ERP, or workflow engine;
- a universal identity system;
- a universal Policy language;
- a blockchain requirement;
- a replacement for Operational Logs;
- a repository of private chain-of-thought or hidden model reasoning;
- proof that every factual assertion inside evidence is true;
- automatic proof of Legal Validity, Regulatory Compliance, accounting correctness, or business wisdom;
- a requirement to publish sensitive financial evidence openly;
- a guarantee that every relevant event was captured;
- a certification program detached from defined versions, roles, profiles, tests, and evidence;
- a proprietary service, API, SDK, or implementation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-017 — Business-truth separation.** TAIP protocol Validity or Verification MUST NOT be represented as automatic proof of business correctness, factual truth, Legal Validity, Regulatory Compliance, or accounting judgment.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-018 — Reasoning boundary.** TAIP MUST NOT require disclosure of private model chain-of-thought as a condition of Core protocol conformance.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-019 — Technology neutrality.** TAIP MUST NOT require a particular blockchain, database, cloud provider, programming language, AI model, vendor, or product unless a separately identified optional binding or profile explicitly selects it.

# 7. Constitutional Protocol Principles

The following principles constrain the entire TAIP series.

## 7.1 Proof, Not Logs

Operational Logs may support evidence creation and investigation, but they are not automatically portable accountability proof.

TAIP evidence is governed by explicit semantics, protected scope, historical state, dependencies, and Verification rules.

## 7.2 Meaning Before Cryptography

Cryptography protects defined input. It does not supply missing object type, role, Authority, Policy, lifecycle, time, or dependency meaning.

## 7.3 Identity Is Not a Key

Protocol Identity, cryptographic key, Key Purpose, Authority, Authorization, and Policy are distinct concepts.

## 7.4 Signature Is Not Authorization

A valid Signature proves protection by a key over defined input. Authorization requires evaluation of applicable identity, Key Purpose, Authority, Policy, and Historical State.

## 7.5 Object Integrity Is Not Historical Integrity

A valid object does not by itself prove timely creation, ordered inclusion, completeness, or absence of a conflicting history.

## 7.6 Correction Adds State

Committed evidence is corrected, superseded, revoked, reassessed, renewed, or migrated through additional attributable state, not silent in-place rewriting.

## 7.7 Replication Is Not Independence

Several instances under common control may improve availability without providing independent assurance.

## 7.8 Storage Is Not Preservation

Future Verification depends on evidence, interpretation dependencies, integrity, availability, recoverability, and historical rules.

## 7.9 Validity Is Not Completeness

Individually valid evidence may remain insufficient for the Accountability Claim or Trust Profile.

## 7.10 Intended Is Not Achieved

Configured or promised assurance does not establish the controls actually supported by evidence.

## 7.11 Uncertainty Is a Result

Invalid, incomplete, indeterminate, unsupported, unavailable, conflicting, warning, and not-evaluated states remain distinct where they affect interpretation.

## 7.12 Protocol Before Product

Normative meaning must remain independently implementable and must not depend on one proprietary service.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-020 — Principle preservation.** Every TAIP specification, profile, Registry, binding, and conformance rule MUST preserve the distinctions in Sections 7.1–7.12.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-021 — Explicit uncertainty.** Missing, malformed, conflicting, unsupported, unavailable, or incomplete mandatory evidence MUST NOT be silently converted into a successful result.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-022 — Append-only correction.** A normative TAIP mechanism affecting committed history MUST represent correction or migration through new attributable state while preserving the original state and relationship.

# 11. Core and Module Boundary

TAIP Core defines the common semantic minimum required to prevent incompatible interpretation across implementations.

Core is expected to govern:

- common Protocol Object envelope and type dispatch;
- version identification and dependency binding;
- identifier and typed-reference foundations;
- Canonical Representation boundaries;
- protected and unprotected field distinction;
- lifecycle-state distinction;
- extension criticality and unknown-value behavior;
- validation-layer and Verification Outcome foundations;
- common conformance-claim structure;
- cross-cutting security and privacy rules.

Modules define object- or mechanism-specific behavior within Core boundaries.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-033 — Core minimum.** Core MUST define every semantic rule required for a consumer to identify, dispatch, preserve, and safely reject or defer an otherwise unknown TAIP Protocol Object.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-034 — Module declaration.** Each module MUST identify its Core dependency, roles, object types, state machines, extension points, Registries, schemas, conformance obligations, security considerations, and privacy considerations.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-035 — No hidden module dependency.** A module MUST NOT depend on another module's semantics without an explicit versioned normative dependency.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-036 — Safe optionality.** Optional module support MUST NOT cause an implementation to misinterpret unsupported mandatory evidence as successfully verified.

# 12. Protocol Object Foundation

All TAIP evidence is represented by, or integrity-bound to, governed Protocol Objects.

A normative Protocol Object definition is expected to identify:

- type identifier and namespace;
- semantic purpose;
- object and schema version;
- producer, issuer, subject, and verifier roles where applicable;
- required, optional, conditional, and prohibited properties;
- object identifier and digest semantics;
- Canonical Representation and cryptographic scope;
- typed references and Verification Dependencies;
- lifecycle states and transitions;
- correction, supersession, revocation, and migration behavior;
- extension locations and criticality;
- validation and Verification Outcomes;
- security and privacy properties;
- positive, negative, boundary, and unsupported test cases.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-037 — Self-describing objects.** A Protocol Object MUST identify or unambiguously bind to the governed type, version, and interpretation rules required for its processing.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-038 — Identifier separation.** Object identifier, object digest, storage locator, and network locator MUST remain distinguishable unless a specific object definition explicitly and safely defines a content-derived identity.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-039 — Canonical determinism.** Equivalent finalized protocol meaning under the same applicable rules MUST produce identical canonical cryptographic input across conforming implementations.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-040 — Protected scope.** A Protocol Object participating in cryptographic operations MUST make protected and unprotected properties distinguishable.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-041 — Critical semantics.** Unknown mandatory or critical semantics MUST produce an explicit non-success outcome and MUST NOT be silently ignored.

# 13. Identity, Keys, Authority, and Policy

TAIP separates accountable identity from cryptographic instruments and permission evidence.

```text
Protocol Identity
      ≠
Cryptographic Key
      ≠
Key Purpose
      ≠
Authority
      ≠
Authorization
      ≠
Policy
```

**TAIP-0000-REQ-042 — Identity/key separation.** A TAIP specification MUST NOT define a cryptographic key as the permanent equivalent of a Protocol Identity.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-043 — Signature/authorization separation.** Signature validity MUST remain distinguishable from Authority and Authorization conclusions.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-044 — Historical key evaluation.** Verification of a historical Signature MUST use the identity, key, Key Purpose, status, Authority, algorithm policy, and time context applicable to the evaluated historical boundary.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-045 — Delegation scope.** Authority delegation and subdelegation MUST identify their scope, effective boundaries, constraints, and accountable source.

No universal identity provider is required. A profile or deployment may select one or more identity and Authority mechanisms when their protocol meaning, historical evidence, and trust assumptions remain explicit.

# 14. Lifecycle and Historical Integrity

TAIP distinguishes lifecycle states whose conflation would overstate accountability.

At minimum, specifications must preserve applicable distinctions among:

```text
Created
Finalized
Signed
Submitted
Accepted
Committed
Witnessed
Checkpointed
Anchored
Preserved
Verified
```

Execution or settlement is also distinct from evidence-state transitions unless an applicable specification explicitly binds them.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-046 — Lifecycle distinction.** Submission, Acceptance, Commitment, Witnessing, Checkpointing, Anchoring, Preservation, execution, and Verification MUST NOT be treated as equivalent states.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-047 — Historical relationship.** A historical mechanism MUST define chain, sequence, predecessor, inclusion, consistency, checkpoint, or other evidence sufficient to evaluate the historical claim it makes.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-048 — No silent rewrite.** Committed content and committed historical relationships MUST NOT be modified in place.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-049 — Explicit conflict.** Forks, equivocation, omission evidence, conflicting Commitments, and incompatible historical state MUST remain visible to Verification.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-050 — Time semantics.** Event time, assertion time, Signature time, receipt time, Commitment time, Witness time, Checkpoint time, anchor time, preservation time, and Verification time MUST remain distinguishable where they affect conclusions.

# 15. Independent Observation and Layered Assurance

TAIP composes controls that protect different properties.

| Control | Principal property |
|---|---|
| Signature | Integrity and signer-key attribution over protected input |
| Hash Chain | Ordered append-only historical relationship |
| Witness Observation | Observation from a defined trust domain |
| Checkpoint | Commitment to a bounded historical state |
| External Anchor | Binding into another trust domain |
| Key Transparency | Historical identity and key state |
| Preservation | Future availability and interpretability |
| Dispute Pack | Portable claim-focused evidence |
| Verification | Reproducible bounded conclusions |
| Trust Profile | Required control composition and achieved assurance |

**TAIP-0000-REQ-051 — Control boundaries.** A TAIP artifact MUST state what property each control establishes and MUST NOT imply properties outside that control's defined scope.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-052 — Independence evaluation.** Witness, anchor, operator, and verifier independence claims MUST identify substantive control domains and correlated-failure assumptions; instance count alone MUST NOT establish independence.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-053 — Evidence-based assurance.** Assurance MUST be calculated from valid available evidence of satisfied controls rather than from configured intent or service branding.

# 16. Trust Profiles and Degraded Mode

Trust Profiles specialize TAIP for different assurance objectives without redefining Core meaning.

A Trust Profile may select:

- mandatory and optional modules;
- object and dependency requirements;
- algorithms, parameters, and key custody;
- Witness eligibility, diversity, thresholds, and timing;
- Checkpoint and anchor requirements;
- Key Transparency and monitoring requirements;
- Preservation duration, redundancy, recovery, and migration controls;
- privacy and disclosure rules;
- fallback, exception, and reassessment behavior;
- conformance suites and evidence.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-054 — Intended/Achieved separation.** An Intended Trust Profile MUST remain distinguishable from the Achieved Trust Profile supported by the evaluated evidence.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-055 — No silent downgrade.** Failure to satisfy a mandatory profile control MUST remain visible and MUST affect the achieved result according to the profile.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-056 — Explicit fallback.** Degraded Mode or fallback behavior MUST identify the triggering condition, permitted actions, resulting evidence, achieved controls, prohibited claims, and reassessment path.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-057 — Core stability.** A Trust Profile MUST NOT redefine Core identifiers, lifecycle states, canonicalization, criticality, or Verification Outcome semantics incompatibly.

# 17. Verification Model

TAIP Verification evaluates governed claims from identified evidence and dependencies under an explicit Verification Context.

A Verification Context is expected to bind:

- focal Accountability Claims;
- historical boundary;
- TAIP Core and module versions;
- Trust Profile version;
- Registry, schema, canonicalization, algorithm, extension, and binding versions;
- available, unavailable, withheld, redacted, and external evidence;
- resolver and trust configuration;
- verification time and policy inputs;
- applicable exceptions and limits.

TAIP Verification Outcomes must preserve material distinctions among:

- valid;
- invalid;
- incomplete;
- indeterminate;
- unsupported;
- unavailable;
- conflicting;
- warning;
- not evaluated;
- profile not achieved.

The exact outcome model belongs in `TAIP-0010` and associated Registries.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-058 — Deterministic outcome.** Conforming Verification Engines given the same governed inputs, dependencies, versions, profile, and parameters MUST produce equivalent protocol conclusions or explicit Unsupported results.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-059 — Validity/completeness separation.** Object Validity, evidence Completeness, and Trust Profile achievement MUST remain separate conclusions.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-060 — Dependency visibility.** A Verification result MUST identify every unavailable, invalid, conflicting, unsupported, or omitted mandatory dependency that materially affected the conclusion.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-061 — Reproducible report.** A normative Verification Report MUST contain or bind sufficient context for another conforming verifier to reproduce or explain the protocol result.

# 18. Privacy and Disclosure

TAIP supports accountability without requiring unrestricted disclosure.

Evidence may use:

- digests and commitments instead of plaintext;
- typed protected references;
- selective disclosure;
- encryption and access controls;
- disclosure-specific Dispute Packs;
- separated payload and metadata custody;
- governed redaction and derivation;
- privacy-aware Witness, Checkpoint, and transparency inputs.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-062 — Data minimization.** TAIP specifications and profiles MUST minimize collection and disclosure to evidence proportionate to the Accountability Claim and assurance objective.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-063 — Explicit derivation.** Redacted, selectively disclosed, transformed, migrated, or otherwise derived evidence MUST remain distinguishable from complete original evidence.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-064 — Privacy/completeness boundary.** Withheld or protected information MUST NOT be treated as present and successfully evaluated merely because its disclosure is restricted.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-065 — Metadata protection.** Specifications MUST evaluate privacy risks arising from identifiers, timestamps, relationship graphs, chain positions, Witness and anchor selection, key history, retrieval, and Verification metadata.

Privacy controls may limit the conclusions available to a verifier. The limitation must be explicit without requiring disclosure of the protected content itself.

# 19. Security Requirements

Every normative TAIP artifact must include security analysis appropriate to its scope.

At minimum, review must consider applicable risks from:

- producer compromise and insider manipulation;
- retrospective evidence creation;
- ambiguous or non-deterministic canonicalization;
- Signature substitution and algorithm confusion;
- key compromise, rotation, and current-state substitution;
- Authority and Policy ambiguity;
- replay, omission, reorder, truncation, fork, and equivocation;
- common-control Witness or anchor failure;
- Checkpoint rollback or anchor misinterpretation;
- dependency deletion and Preservation failure;
- malicious, incomplete, or selectively assembled Dispute Packs;
- unsupported critical extensions and version downgrade;
- verifier divergence, capture, or resource exhaustion;
- Registry, release, dependency, and software supply-chain compromise;
- privacy leakage and unauthorized disclosure;
- migration that overwrites original evidence or historical meaning.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-066 — Security analysis.** A normative TAIP artifact MUST document its trust assumptions, protected assets, attacker capabilities, failure modes, and required mitigations.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-067 — Fail-safe semantics.** Security-sensitive ambiguity, unsupported mandatory semantics, invalid cryptographic evidence, or unresolved required historical state MUST NOT produce a successful Verification result.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-068 — Cryptographic agility.** Cryptographic algorithms, suites, parameters, and status MUST be explicitly identified and governable without silently changing historical interpretation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-069 — Resource bounds.** Parsers, resolvers, proof evaluators, package processors, and Verification Engines MUST define safe behavior for excessive size, depth, recursion, references, decompression, computation, and network retrieval.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-070 — Trust-domain visibility.** A protocol claim relying on multiple actors or services MUST preserve the control-domain assumptions relevant to independence and correlated failure.

# 20. Versioning and Compatibility

TAIP uses independent artifact versions. Semantic versioning is the default for specifications whose public contract can be classified meaningfully as major, minor, and patch.

For such artifacts:

- **major** changes alter a required contract incompatibly or change a material semantic boundary;
- **minor** changes add compatible optional or negotiated behavior without changing prior required meaning;
- **patch** changes correct defects or clarify uniquely intended behavior without changing the valid contract.

A version number does not by itself prove compatibility.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-071 — Version immutability.** Published normative content for an artifact ID and version MUST be immutable.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-072 — Compatibility scope.** A compatibility claim MUST identify compared artifacts and versions, direction, roles, features, profiles, extensions, bindings, assumptions, losses, and test evidence.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-073 — Semantic classification.** A change to canonicalization, protected scope, identifier meaning, lifecycle state, criticality, outcome composition, historical resolution, or mandatory security behavior MUST be classified by semantic impact rather than by wire-shape similarity alone.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-074 — Historical version binding.** Evidence and Verification Reports MUST bind or resolve every version dimension material to their interpretation and MUST NOT rely solely on a mutable `latest` or `current` reference.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-075 — Migration non-rewrite.** Migration to a new object, schema, algorithm, profile, or package version MUST preserve the original evidence and create an attributable relationship between source and target state.

---

# 21. Capability Negotiation and Downgrade Prevention

Negotiation may select supported versions, modules, profiles, representations, algorithms, extensions, and bindings.

Negotiation itself is accountability-relevant when it changes assurance or interpretation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-076 — Capability declaration.** A capability declaration MUST identify the declaring role, applicable versions, supported features, constraints, status, and freshness or effective boundary.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-077 — No silent downgrade.** Negotiation MUST NOT select a weaker version, algorithm, profile, extension behavior, or binding when the applicable policy prohibits that downgrade.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-078 — Negotiation evidence.** A material negotiated choice affecting Accountability Claims or Achieved Trust Profile MUST be represented in, or integrity-bound to, the resulting evidence.

Absence of a capability declaration is not evidence of support. A parser's ability to accept bytes is not evidence of semantic compatibility.

---

# 22. Extension Policy

TAIP permits governed extension without allowing extensions to redefine Core.

Every extension must define:

- namespace and stable identifier;
- version and authority;
- scope and permitted locations;
- critical or non-critical status;
- Canonical Representation and protected scope;
- producer and consumer behavior;
- validation and Verification Outcomes;
- compatibility and negotiation;
- security and privacy considerations;
- lifecycle, deprecation, and preservation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-079 — Critical extension handling.** An implementation that does not understand a critical extension MUST produce an explicit Unsupported result for every conclusion whose meaning may depend on that extension.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-080 — Non-critical extension safety.** An unknown non-critical extension MAY be preserved and ignored only when the governing specification establishes that ignoring it cannot change mandatory interpretation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-081 — Namespace safety.** Extension identifiers MUST be governed within an explicit namespace and MUST NOT collide with or impersonate Core identifiers.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-082 — Core non-redefinition.** An extension MUST NOT alter the meaning of a Core field, state, identifier, algorithm, outcome, or requirement.

---

# 23. Registry Policy

Registries assign governed values and metadata within authority delegated by a specification.

A TAIP Registry must define:

- Registry identity and version or revision model;
- governing authority and operator;
- entry schema and allocation policy;
- uniqueness and namespace rules;
- semantic scope of each entry;
- status, activation, deprecation, withdrawal, and replacement;
- criticality and unknown-value behavior;
- compatibility and security review;
- integrity, publication, mirroring, and historical preservation;
- appeal and conflict handling.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-083 — Registry authority.** A Registry entry MUST NOT create semantics beyond the extension point and authority delegated by its governing specification.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-084 — No identifier reuse.** A published Registry identifier MUST NOT be reassigned to incompatible meaning.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-085 — Historical Registry state.** Registry state required for historical Verification MUST remain identifiable, integrity-verifiable, and preservable.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-086 — Unknown values.** The governing specification MUST define safe behavior for unknown, deprecated, withdrawn, reserved, and experimental Registry values.

---

# 24. Conformance and Testability

TAIP conformance is role-, artifact-, version-, feature-, profile-, and binding-specific.

A conformance claim must identify:

- implementation and build;
- implemented role;
- TAIP Core and module versions;
- supported Protocol Object types;
- profiles, Registries, schemas, canonicalization rules, bindings, and extensions;
- algorithms and optional features;
- conformance suite and test-vector versions;
- environment and configuration;
- executed, passed, failed, skipped, unsupported, and not-applicable tests;
- deviations, waivers, and known limitations;
- report identity, producer, integrity, and time.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-087 — Test mapping.** Every independently testable normative rule MUST map to one or more conformance assertions or an explicit justified non-testable disposition.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-088 — Negative coverage.** Conformance suites MUST include invalid, incomplete, unsupported, unavailable, conflicting, boundary, downgrade, and resource-limit cases where applicable, not only successful examples.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-089 — Scoped result.** Passing a parser, producer, verifier, module, profile, or binding subset MUST NOT be represented as full TAIP conformance.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-090 — Reference-code boundary.** Reference implementations, SDKs, examples, and generated code MUST NOT become normative merely because they are widely used.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-091 — Reproducible evidence.** A conformance execution MUST produce sufficient versioned evidence to explain the evaluated scope, observed results, omissions, and limitations.

# 26. Governance and Authority

TAIP governance separates decision authority from technical access.

Relevant roles may include:

- Project Bible Authority;
- TAIP Authority;
- module maintainers;
- Profile Authorities;
- Registry Authorities and operators;
- schema and binding maintainers;
- Conformance Authority;
- security response team;
- release manager;
- editors, reviewers, contributors, and implementers;
- appeal or independent review body.

One organization may hold several roles, but each action must remain attributable to the authority acting within a defined mandate.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-096 — Bounded authority.** A normative change, release, Registry allocation, interpretation, erratum, or emergency action MUST identify the responsible authority, mandate, scope, and effective boundary.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-097 — Access/authority separation.** Repository, domain, package, Registry, signing-key, or deployment access MUST NOT by itself establish governance authority.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-098 — Conflict visibility.** Material conflicts of interest, recusals, dissent, and appeals affecting a normative decision MUST be recorded according to the applicable governance policy.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-099 — Governance accountability.** Changes to TAIP governance MUST themselves follow a versioned, attributable, reviewable, and preserved process.

The initial TAIP Authority is the authority publishing Draft artifacts in the canonical repository. Before a stable `1.0.0` release, a dedicated governance record must define maintainers, approval thresholds, appeals, release signing, security response, and continuity.

# 27. Change Process

Normative TAIP changes proceed through accountable stages.

```text
Proposal
   ▼
Scope and Architecture Mapping
   ▼
Draft Normative Text
   ▼
Security, Privacy, and Compatibility Review
   ▼
Implementation and Test Evidence
   ▼
Public Review and Objection Resolution
   ▼
Approval
   ▼
Versioned Publication and Preservation
```

A change proposal should identify:

- problem and motivation;
- affected artifacts, roles, objects, and profiles;
- affected `GINV-*`, `GREQ-*`, and TAIP requirements;
- proposed normative behavior;
- alternatives and rejected options;
- compatibility and migration consequences;
- security and privacy impact;
- conformance and test plan;
- deployment and operational impact;
- unresolved issues;
- requested authority and release class.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-100 — Material-change review.** A material normative change MUST undergo architectural, interoperability, security, privacy, compatibility, and testability review before release.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-101 — Evidence before release.** Critical normative behavior MUST have implementation or test evidence sufficient to demonstrate that independent interpretation is practical before stable publication.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-102 — Objection disposition.** Material review objections MUST be resolved, accepted with documented risk, or explicitly deferred by an authorized decision before publication.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-103 — No hidden release placeholders.** A stable release MUST NOT depend on unresolved placeholders, undocumented mandatory algorithms, or ambiguous required behavior.

RFCs propose material changes. ADRs preserve important decisions and rationale. Neither becomes normative protocol text until incorporated through the governed release process.

---

# 28. Errata, Interpretations, and Emergency Changes

An **erratum** corrects an editorial or technical defect without silently creating an incompatible contract.

A **normative interpretation** resolves ambiguity within the valid meaning of an existing specification.

An **emergency change** addresses an urgent security or continuity risk under bounded temporary authority.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-104 — Errata boundary.** An erratum MUST NOT be used to introduce a materially incompatible rule that requires a new major version.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-105 — Interpretation record.** A normative interpretation MUST identify the ambiguity, affected versions and requirements, authority, rationale, effective boundary, test impact, and relationship to future text.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-106 — Emergency limits.** Emergency authority MUST be time-bounded, scope-bounded, attributable, and followed by ordinary review, ratification, replacement, or expiry.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-107 — Historical preservation.** Errata, interpretations, advisories, and emergency changes MUST preserve the original published artifact and the attributable relationship to later guidance or replacement.

---

# 29. Publication and Preservation

A normative TAIP release is a governed artifact set, not merely a mutable web page.

A release should publish:

- artifact IDs, versions, status, and authoritative locations;
- content digests and release manifest;
- incorporated normative dependencies;
- applicable Project Bible edition;
- schemas, Registries, canonicalization rules, and bindings;
- test vectors and conformance-suite versions;
- errata and normative interpretations;
- compatibility and migration declarations;
- authority and approval evidence;
- preservation and mirror information.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-108 — Release manifest.** A stable TAIP release MUST include an integrity-bound manifest identifying every normative artifact and version required for interpretation.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-109 — Historical availability.** Superseded, deprecated, withdrawn, or vulnerable normative artifacts required to interpret historical evidence MUST remain preservable and distinguishable from artifacts approved for new use.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-110 — Canonical history.** Corrections, replacements, and migrations MUST create new published state and MUST NOT overwrite the historical release record.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-111 — Mirror verification.** A mirror or alternate package MUST provide a deterministic and integrity-verifiable relationship to the authoritative release content.

---

# 30. Release Readiness

A TAIP artifact is ready for stable normative publication only when:

- scope, authority, status, and dependencies are explicit;
- normative and informative material are distinguishable;
- mandatory semantics are complete and unambiguous;
- architecture mapping and global-requirement disposition are reviewed;
- identifiers, versions, schemas, Registries, and examples are consistent;
- positive, negative, boundary, unsupported, and downgrade tests exist for critical rules;
- independent implementation review has occurred where practical;
- security and privacy review is complete;
- compatibility, migration, and deprecation behavior are documented;
- unresolved material is bounded and does not control mandatory behavior;
- the release manifest and preservation plan are complete;
- conformance claims can be stated precisely.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-112 — Readiness gate.** An artifact failing a mandatory release-readiness condition MUST remain Draft, Experimental, or otherwise explicitly non-stable.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-113 — Stable claim evidence.** A stable conformance claim MUST bind to an approved release and applicable conformance evidence; repository presence or implementation availability alone is insufficient.

# 31. Implementation Independence

TAIP must support independent clean-room implementation from normative artifacts.

Implementers may build:

- embedded libraries;
- local command-line tools;
- managed services;
- gateways;
- federated services;
- offline verifiers;
- hardware-backed components;
- open-source or proprietary products.

Different architectures are acceptable when they preserve normative semantics and conformance behavior.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-114 — Independent implementation.** A normative TAIP feature MUST be implementable without access to proprietary source code, private service behavior, or undisclosed test data.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-115 — API/SDK boundary.** An API, SDK, generated type, or product convenience MUST expose mandatory protocol states and failures faithfully and MUST NOT redefine normative object identity, lifecycle, or Verification semantics.

**TAIP-0000-REQ-116 — Vendor exit.** Protocol Evidence and required Verification Dependencies SHOULD remain exportable in a form that permits authorized independent Verification after a producer or service is replaced.

# 32. Security Considerations

The charter's security objective is not to declare TAIP secure by design label. It is to require every security-relevant assumption and failure boundary to become explicit, testable, and historically interpretable.

## 32.1 Producer Control

The Evidence Producer may control operational systems, clocks, credentials, retention, exports, and narrative. TAIP reduces—but cannot eliminate—this dependency through Canonical Representation, cryptographic protection, append-only Commitment, independent observation, Checkpoints, anchors, Historical Key State, Preservation, and Verification.

Profiles must select controls appropriate to the producer threat model.

## 32.2 Cryptographic Substitution

Algorithm identifiers, parameters, Key Purpose, protected scope, canonicalization, and target object must be bound unambiguously. Algorithm agility must not permit downgrade or cross-protocol substitution.

## 32.3 Historical Attacks

Backdating, replay, omission, reorder, fork, equivocation, rollback, and current-state substitution require distinct evidence and outcomes. A valid Signature over a valid object is insufficient to establish historical continuity.

## 32.4 Independence Theater

Multiple services or organizations may share ownership, administrators, credentials, infrastructure, release pipelines, or legal control. Conformance and profile evidence should identify substantive trust domains rather than count labels.

## 32.5 Dependency Attacks

Mutable URLs, unavailable schemas, replaced Registry entries, missing profiles, changed canonicalization rules, and unpreserved key history can change or prevent Verification. Normative dependencies require stable identity, versions, integrity, and preservation.

## 32.6 Parser and Verifier Attacks

Protocol processing may encounter malicious nesting, cycles, duplicate keys, ambiguous numbers, invalid Unicode, oversized proofs, decompression bombs, resolver loops, algorithm abuse, or network resource exhaustion. Each applicable specification must define bounded and fail-safe behavior.

## 32.7 Governance and Supply Chain

Repository compromise, release-key theft, malicious package publication, generated-artifact drift, Registry capture, and conformance-suite manipulation can change effective behavior. Release manifests, review evidence, protected publishing, reproducible generation, stable identifiers, and preserved history are security controls.

## 32.8 Overclaiming

The most persistent semantic attack is presenting a bounded protocol result as proof of broader truth. Structured outcomes and explicit claim scope are mandatory defenses.

---

# 33. Privacy Considerations

TAIP accountability evidence may expose financial actions, identities, relationships, Policies, timing, business strategy, system topology, and investigative interest.

Every module, profile, binding, Registry, and conformance artifact must evaluate:

- data minimization;
- payload and metadata sensitivity;
- correlation and linkability;
- selective disclosure and redaction;
- encryption and key custody;
- access and purpose limitation;
- retention, deletion, and legal obligations;
- resolver and Verification query privacy;
- public archive and Registry exposure;
- cross-organizational disclosure;
- how privacy limits affect Completeness and Achieved Trust Profile.

Preservation does not require unrestricted public availability. Historical integrity does not require retaining every plaintext field. A commitment may preserve evidence of prior state while lawful removal or restricted custody limits later disclosure.

Privacy behavior that changes Verification must remain explicit and testable.

---

# 34. Operational Considerations

TAIP separates protocol semantics from deployment choices, but implementers must define operational behavior that preserves those semantics.

Important concerns include:

- synchronous, asynchronous, and hybrid evidence creation;
- idempotency and duplicate Accountable Actions;
- retry safety;
- partial failure and recovery;
- clock quality and time-source evidence;
- network partition and delayed Commitment;
- Witness, Checkpoint, anchor, transparency, and archive unavailability;
- batching and ordering;
- storage quotas and evidence-lifetime policy;
- resolver caching and historical consistency;
- key rotation and emergency revocation;
- migration and rollback;
- observability without confusing logs with protocol evidence.

Operational success must not be represented as a stronger protocol state than the evidence supports.
