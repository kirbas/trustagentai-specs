## Abstract

This document defines the TrustAgentAI Interoperability Protocol (TAIP) **Execution Evidence Record** and **Execution Evidence Transition Record**.

An Execution Evidence Record is a finalized, attributable Protocol Object representing one primary execution-related event. It may state that an action was requested, received, accepted, rejected, started, progressed, completed, failed, cancelled, produced a result, settled, or was reversed. It identifies the reporting party, relevant Actors, the logical operation and attempt, the accountable action, causal predecessors, inputs, outputs, status, result, error, temporal context, external-system references, Authority and Policy context, and cryptographic protection.

Execution Evidence is a bounded statement by its issuer. It is not automatic proof that the action reflected valid [Intent](TAIP-0001.md), was semantically [Accepted](TAIP-0002.md), was Authorized, produced the asserted real-world effect, achieved settlement finality, entered committed history, or is legally or commercially correct.

An Execution Evidence Transition Record adds accountable correction, supersession, annotation, or dispute state. A reversal is represented as a new execution event because it is an operational action, not a historical rewrite.

Version `0.1.0` defines the normative logical model and validation behavior needed for review and experimental implementation. Stable wire-format, Canonical Representation, algorithm Registry, schema, event Registry, media-type, and common-envelope bindings remain Draft dependencies and must be completed before stable `1.0.0` conformance.

# 1. Status and Applicability

`TAIP-0003` is the third subject-matter specification in the TAIP series.

It applies when an Agent, workflow, tool, service, financial institution, network, counterparty, execution platform, reconciliation system, or other accountable party creates portable evidence about an execution-related event.

Representative uses include:

- payment, transfer, purchase, refund, or settlement processing;
- tool, API, service, and Agent invocation;
- execution acceptance or rejection by an external system;
- partial, multi-stage, asynchronous, or long-running operations;
- failure, timeout, cancellation, retry, and recovery;
- reconciliation and settlement reporting;
- reversal or compensating action;
- execution evidence used in a Dispute Pack or independent Verification.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-001 — Specification applicability.** An object claiming conformance as a TAIP Execution Evidence Record or Execution Evidence Transition Record MUST satisfy this specification and all incorporated normative dependencies.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-002 — Draft claim boundary.** An implementation using version `0.1.0` MUST identify the specification as Draft and MUST NOT present experimental interoperability as stable TAIP conformance.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-003 — Role-scoped claims.** A conformance claim MUST identify whether the implementation acts as an Evidence Producer, issuer, signer, parser, validator, resolver, state evaluator, packager, or verifier.

# 3. Execution Evidence Definition

An **Execution Event** is one accountability-relevant occurrence in the process of requesting, attempting, performing, reporting, settling, failing, cancelling, or reversing an action.

An **Execution Evidence Record** is the finalized Protocol Object carrying one bounded assertion about one primary Execution Event.

```text
Operational Event
      │ observed or reported by
      ▼
Evidence Producer / Issuer
      │
      ▼
Finalized Execution Evidence Record
      │
      ├── may reference Intent and Acceptance
      ├── may reference prior execution events
      ├── may bind external-system evidence
      └── may later be committed, witnessed, or verified
```

The record proves that an attributable issuer made the protected statement when its cryptographic and identity evidence validates. Whether the underlying operational assertion is true requires provenance, causal, external, historical, and possibly independent evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-007 — Bounded assertion.** Every Execution Evidence Record MUST define a bounded assertion whose primary event, issuer, action, operation, attempt, status, and time semantics can be identified independently of the originating application.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-008 — One primary event.** A record MUST represent one primary execution-related event unless a governed composite event type defines atomic component and outcome semantics.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-009 — Reporter boundary.** A valid record establishes an attributable issuer statement and MUST NOT be represented as automatic proof that the issuer directly observed, caused, controlled, or independently verified the underlying event.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-010 — Outcome boundary.** A valid Execution Evidence Record MUST NOT by itself establish Authorization, business correctness, settlement finality, Commitment, evidence Completeness, or Legal Validity.

# 4. Scope and Non-Goals

TAIP-0003 defines:

- Execution Evidence event classes and status semantics;
- logical operation, attempt, record, and external-reference identity;
- action, request, result, error, and effect representation;
- causal binding to Intent, Acceptance, Authorization, Policy, and prior execution evidence;
- partial, multi-stage, batch, retry, concurrency, and idempotency behavior;
- deterministic protected representation and issuer proofs;
- append-only correction, supersession, annotation, and dispute evidence;
- state reconstruction, validation, Verification, portability, and conformance.

TAIP-0003 does not define:

- a universal execution API or transport;
- a universal financial-network or settlement protocol;
- one business-action, result, or error ontology;
- automatic trust in external transaction identifiers;
- guaranteed execution, delivery, settlement, or irreversibility;
- a universal legal definition of completion or finality;
- disclosure of private model chain-of-thought;
- one database, event bus, workflow engine, or vendor architecture.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-011 — Scope preservation.** A TAIP-0003 implementation MUST distinguish execution request, receipt, acceptance, rejection, attempt, completion, result, settlement, reversal, Commitment, and Verification semantics.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-012 — No universal business ontology.** TAIP-0003 MUST NOT require one universal action, status, result, error, or settlement ontology; governed domain vocabularies MAY be incorporated through Registries, profiles, or extensions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-013 — No reasoning disclosure.** Core Execution Evidence conformance MUST NOT require private model chain-of-thought or unrestricted internal model state.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-014 — Operational separation.** TAIP-0003 specifies evidence about execution and MUST NOT be interpreted as requiring TAIP itself to perform or control the business action.

# 5. Roles

TAIP-0003 distinguishes who created the record, who stands behind its assertion, who requested the action, who performed it, and who reported its result.

| Role | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Evidence Producer | Component constructing the Execution Evidence Record |
| Issuer / Reporter | Protocol Identity taking responsibility for the bounded statement |
| Principal | Protocol Identity on whose behalf the issuer or Actor operates |
| Requesting Actor | Human, Agent, workflow, or service requesting execution |
| Executor | Actor claimed to have attempted or performed the action |
| Execution System | System receiving, processing, or reporting the operation |
| Counterparty | External participant materially involved in the action or result |
| Subject | Account, asset, instrument, object, Policy, or person affected |
| Beneficiary | Participant receiving a defined benefit where applicable |
| Result Authority | System or party authoritative for a defined result dimension |
| Settlement Authority | System or party authoritative for a defined settlement state |
| Signer | Key-controlling role producing a Signature or proof |
| Resolver | Component resolving identities, keys, references, schemas, and external evidence |
| State Evaluator | Component reconstructing effective execution state |
| Verifier | Role evaluating the evidence under an explicit Verification Context |

One Protocol Identity may occupy several roles. Role coincidence must remain explicit where it affects Authority, provenance, independence, conflicts of interest, or interpretation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-015 — Issuer presence.** Every Execution Evidence Record MUST identify exactly one logical issuer responsible for the bounded assertion.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-016 — Principal presence.** Every Execution Evidence Record MUST identify exactly one logical principal or explicitly state `not_applicable` under governed rules.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-017 — Producer visibility.** The Evidence Producer MUST be identified when it differs from the issuer or when required by the applicable Trust Profile.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-018 — Actor visibility.** The Requesting Actor, Executor, Execution System, Result Authority, Settlement Authority, subject, counterparty, and beneficiary MUST be identified when their roles are material to the event or claim.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-019 — Role separation.** Producer, issuer, principal, requester, Executor, Execution System, Result Authority, Settlement Authority, signer, Registry, and verifier MUST NOT be presumed identical merely because one Protocol Identity appears in several fields.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-020 — Signer role.** Every Signature or equivalent proof MUST identify the role in which its signer acts and the protected statement for which that role is asserted.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-021 — Source-of-knowledge.** The issuer MUST identify whether its assertion is based on direct operation, direct observation, authenticated external response, derived processing, reconciliation, Human report, or another governed source class.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-022 — Independence boundary.** Evidence produced by the Executor or a system in the same control domain MUST NOT be represented as independently corroborated without separate independence evidence.

# 6. Execution Event Classes

`event_type` identifies the primary event asserted by the record.

Initial event types are:

| Value | Primary assertion |
|---|---|
| `execution_request` | A defined action was submitted for execution |
| `execution_receipt` | An execution endpoint received the request |
| `execution_acceptance` | An execution system accepted the request for defined processing |
| `execution_rejection` | An execution system rejected the request |
| `execution_start` | Processing or performance began |
| `execution_progress` | A defined intermediate stage or progress state occurred |
| `execution_completion` | The execution system reports terminal processing completion |
| `execution_failure` | A defined execution stage failed |
| `execution_cancellation` | A defined request or attempt was cancelled |
| `result_report` | A defined output or business effect was reported |
| `settlement_report` | A defined settlement state was reported |
| `reversal` | A new action attempted or achieved reversal or compensation of an earlier effect |

The `execution_acceptance` event is distinct from the Acceptance Record defined by TAIP-0002. TAIP-0002 records a responder's decision about an Intent; `execution_acceptance` records an execution system's response to an Execution Request.

Correction, supersession, annotation, and dispute of evidence use the transition object in Section 29 rather than being represented as operational execution events.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-023 — Governed event type.** `event_type` MUST use a value defined by this specification or an incorporated governed Registry or critical extension.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-024 — Permitted status set.** Each event type definition MUST identify its permitted `event_status` values and required event-specific properties.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-025 — Request boundary.** `execution_request` MUST assert only that a request was sent or invoked under the defined semantics and MUST NOT imply receipt, acceptance, execution, or result.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-026 — Receipt boundary.** `execution_receipt` MUST assert only the governed receipt fact and MUST NOT imply semantic acceptance or processing success.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-027 — Acceptance boundary.** `execution_acceptance` MUST identify what the execution system accepted for processing and MUST NOT imply completion, business effect, settlement, or TAIP Evidence Commitment.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-028 — Completion boundary.** `execution_completion` MUST identify what processing stage completed and MUST NOT imply settlement, irreversibility, or business correctness unless those are separate protected assertions supported by the event type.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-029 — Settlement boundary.** `settlement_report` MUST define the settlement state, scope, reporter, finality semantics, reversibility, and supporting source rather than relying on the word `settled` alone.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-030 — Unknown event.** An unknown critical event type MUST produce Unsupported and MUST NOT be coerced to a generic success, failure, or result event.

For this version:

Execution Evidence ID
≠ Execution ID
≠ Attempt ID
≠ Intent ID
≠ Acceptance ID
≠ External Operation ID
≠ Idempotency Key
≠ Digest
≠ Storage Locator

The initial logical identifier syntax is URI-form under a governed namespace.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-038 — Exact interpretation.** A verifier MUST evaluate a record under the normative versions identified by the protected object and MUST NOT silently substitute current or `latest` rules.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-043 — No content ambiguity.** The same `execution_evidence_id` MUST NOT identify different finalized protected content.

# 9. Execution, Attempt, Sequence, and Idempotency Identity

`execution_identity` distinguishes the logical operation from its attempts and event records.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `execution_id` | 1 | Stable identifier of the logical operation or governed execution lineage |
| `attempt_id` | 1 | Stable identifier of one execution attempt |
| `attempt_number` | 0..1 | Responder- or workflow-scoped attempt ordinal |
| `event_sequence` | 0..1 | Governed sequence within the attempt or reporting namespace |
| `idempotency_key` | 0..1 | Key used by a defined processor to suppress duplicate effects |
| `idempotency_scope` | 0..1 | Processor, action, principal, time, and comparison scope of the key |

An `execution_id` does not prove that every related event concerns the same real-world action. Relationship, issuer, action, and integrity evidence must still be evaluated.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-046 — Required operation identity.** Every record MUST identify one logical `execution_id` and one `attempt_id` under governed namespaces.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-047 — Attempt distinction.** A new accountability-relevant attempt MUST receive a distinct `attempt_id`, even when the `execution_id`, action, workflow, or idempotency key is reused.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-048 — Record distinction.** Each primary event in an attempt MUST use a distinct `execution_evidence_id`; an operation or attempt identifier MUST NOT substitute for the evidence-record identifier.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-049 — Sequence semantics.** An `attempt_number` or `event_sequence` MUST identify its issuing namespace, monotonicity, reset, gap, concurrency, and comparison rules.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-050 — No sequence overclaim.** A local sequence MUST NOT be represented as global causality or trusted time without supporting evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-051 — Idempotency semantics.** An idempotency key MUST identify the processor, comparison rules, scope, lifetime, protected inputs, and intended duplicate effect.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-052 — Idempotency boundary.** Equality of idempotency keys MUST NOT be interpreted as equality of evidence records, attempts, external operations, or business effects.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-053 — Identity collision.** Conflicting execution, attempt, sequence, or idempotency claims MUST remain visible and MUST produce a bounded state result.

# 10. Accountable Action and Request Binding

`action` describes the action to which the event relates.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `action_type` | 1 | Governed action classification |
| `targets` | 0..n | Typed affected accounts, assets, objects, services, or participants |
| `parameters` | 1 | Governed structured parameters, possibly empty |
| `requested_state_change` | 0..1 | Bounded prior-to-intended state transition |
| `component_model` | 0..1 | Governed composite-action structure |
| `human_summary` | 0..1 | Informative display text |

For `execution_request`, the action is the requested action. For later events, the record must identify whether the reported action is exact, narrowed, transformed, partially applied, or inconsistent with the request.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-054 — Governed action.** `action_type`, parameter schema, target semantics, units, and component model MUST be defined by an incorporated normative source.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-055 — Structured meaning.** Information required to evaluate the action MUST use governed structured properties and MUST NOT depend solely on free-form text.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-056 — Target typing.** Each action target MUST identify its role, type or namespace, stable identity, and required integrity expectation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-057 — Request binding.** Every event after `execution_request` MUST strongly reference the applicable Execution Request Evidence Record or explicitly state why no TAIP request record is available or applicable.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-058 — Action comparison.** A verifier evaluating conformance to a request MUST compare protected action semantics rather than relying on workflow IDs, summaries, external transaction IDs, or approximate display values.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-059 — Transformation visibility.** Any narrowing, enrichment, normalization, conversion, routing, substitution, or transformation of the requested action MUST be explicit and governed.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-060 — Exact values.** Financial amounts, quantities, rates, currencies, assets, units, rounding, precision, and conversion rules MUST use exact governed representations.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-061 — Summary boundary.** `human_summary` MUST be treated as informative unless its exact content is explicitly part of the governed protected semantics.

# 11. Causal Context and Predecessor Binding

`causal_context` connects execution evidence to the decisions and events that precede or affect it.

It contains typed references with explicit statuses for:

- target Intent Record;
- applicable Acceptance Record;
- Authorization or approval evidence;
- Authority and delegation evidence;
- Policy and Policy-evaluation evidence;
- Execution Request Evidence Record;
- prior event in the same attempt;
- parent, component, retry, compensation, or reversal event;
- external source or reconciliation evidence.

Each dependency status is one of `strongly_referenced`, `referenced`, `embedded`, `not_asserted`, `not_applicable`, `unavailable`, `withheld`, or `unsupported` according to governed rules.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-062 — Typed causality.** Every accountability-critical causal relationship MUST identify its governed relationship, expected target type and version, target identity, and required integrity strength.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-063 — Strong predecessor.** A predecessor whose substitution could change the meaning, Authorization, requested action, attempt, result, or effective state MUST be bound by stable identifier and canonical digest.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-064 — Intent boundary.** A reference to an Intent Record establishes a claimed causal relationship and MUST NOT by itself prove the Intent was valid, current, accepted, or authorized.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-065 — Acceptance boundary.** A reference to an Acceptance Record MUST identify its exact protected decision and scope and MUST NOT be treated as Execution Evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-066 — Authorization boundary.** Authorization or approval evidence MUST remain distinct from Intent, Acceptance, request, execution, result, and settlement evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-067 — Missing predecessor.** A missing, unavailable, withheld, invalid, conflicted, or unsupported mandatory predecessor MUST remain explicit and MUST affect completeness and profile conclusions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-068 — Correlation boundary.** A workflow, conversation, case, transaction, session, message, or correlation identifier MUST NOT by itself establish causality.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-069 — Graph control.** Resolvers and verifiers MUST enforce governed cycle, graph-depth, reference-count, retrieval-size, and processing-time limits.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-070 — No causal invention.** A producer or verifier MUST NOT invent a missing predecessor, causal order, or Authorization relationship to complete an otherwise incomplete graph.

# 12. Event Status and Outcome

`event_status` states the status of the one primary event under its governed event type.

Representative values include:

- `requested`;
- `received`;
- `accepted_for_processing`;
- `rejected`;
- `started`;
- `pending`;
- `partially_completed`;
- `completed`;
- `failed`;
- `cancelled`;
- `settlement_pending`;
- `settled`;
- `reversed`;
- `unknown`.

```text
Requested
≠ Received
≠ Accepted for Processing
≠ Started
≠ Completed
≠ Result Confirmed
≠ Settled
≠ Irreversible
```

**TAIP-0003-REQ-071 — Governed status.** `event_status` MUST be permitted and precisely defined for the protected `event_type` and version.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-072 — Stage specificity.** A status MUST identify the execution or settlement stage to which it applies and MUST NOT be interpreted as a broader workflow status.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-073 — Pending semantics.** `pending` MUST identify the awaited event, responsible system or role, applicable deadline or review boundary when known, and whether processing may continue.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-074 — Completion semantics.** `completed` MUST identify what completed, the success criteria used, and any remaining downstream result, settlement, reconciliation, or finality dependencies.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-075 — Failure semantics.** `failed` MUST identify the failed stage and MUST NOT imply that no external or partial effect occurred.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-076 — Cancellation semantics.** `cancelled` MUST identify the cancelled request, attempt, component, or future processing scope and MUST NOT erase effects that occurred before cancellation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-077 — Unknown status.** `unknown` MUST identify why the status is unknown and MUST NOT be mapped to success, failure, or absence of effect.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-078 — External vocabulary mapping.** Mapping an external status into `event_status` MUST be explicit, versioned, testable, and bounded to the source evidence.

# 13. Results, Effects, Settlement, and Finality

`result` represents bounded claims about outputs or effects of the primary event.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `reported`, `confirmed_by_source`, `reconciled`, `disputed`, `unknown`, `not_applicable`, or `unavailable` |
| `result_type` | 0..1 | Governed result classification |
| `values` | 0..n | Structured result values or integrity-bound references |
| `state_before` | 0..1 | Claimed prior state |
| `state_after` | 0..1 | Claimed resulting state |
| `effect_scope` | 0..1 | Whole action or explicit components affected |
| `finality` | 0..1 | Governed finality level and source |
| `reversibility` | 0..1 | Whether and under what rules reversal remains possible |
| `supporting_refs` | 0..n | External, reconciliation, or independent evidence |

**TAIP-0003-REQ-079 — Explicit result status.** Every record MUST state one governed `result.status`, including `not_applicable` when the event makes no result claim.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-080 — Source attribution.** Every material result or effect claim MUST identify whether it was produced, returned, observed, derived, reconciled, or asserted by an external source.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-081 — Result typing.** Result values, state transitions, units, rounding, precision, comparison, and success criteria MUST use governed semantics.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-082 — Result/occurrence separation.** Integrity of a result report MUST NOT be interpreted as proof that the reported real-world effect occurred exactly as stated.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-083 — Settlement semantics.** A settlement claim MUST identify the authoritative source, settlement rail or domain, state, scope, time semantics, finality level, reversibility, and supporting evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-084 — Finality boundary.** Finality labels such as `final`, `irreversible`, or `confirmed` MUST use governed domain definitions and MUST NOT exceed what the supporting source establishes.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-085 — Reversal boundary.** A reversal, refund, chargeback, cancellation, or compensating action MUST be represented as additional execution evidence and MUST NOT make the original event disappear.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-086 — Disputed result.** Conflicting, corrected, or disputed result evidence MUST remain visible and MUST produce a bounded conclusion rather than silent replacement.

# 14. Rejection, Error, and Failure Information

`error` represents a rejection, failure, warning, or limitation relevant to the event.

| Property | Cardinality | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `status` | 1 | `present`, `not_present`, `unknown`, `withheld`, or `not_applicable` |
| `stage` | 0..1 | Dispatch, transport, validation, authorization, acceptance, execution, result, settlement, reconciliation, or other governed stage |
| `code` | 0..1 | Governed error or rejection code |
| `category` | 0..1 | Permanent, transient, policy, authority, input, external, timeout, conflict, or other governed class |
| `retryability` | 0..1 | Governed retry guidance |
| `effect_state` | 0..1 | `none_known`, `possible`, `partial`, `confirmed`, or `unknown` |
| `details` | 0..1 | Structured details or integrity-bound reference |
| `human_summary` | 0..1 | Informative explanation |

**TAIP-0003-REQ-087 — Explicit error status.** Every record MUST state one governed `error.status` value.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-088 — Failure location.** A rejection or failure MUST identify the affected stage when known and MUST NOT be generalized to unrelated workflow stages.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-089 — Effect uncertainty.** A timeout, network error, or lost response MUST NOT be interpreted as evidence that no external effect occurred.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-093 — No success masking.** A successful envelope, batch, retry, later stage, or compensating action MUST NOT conceal or rewrite an earlier record-level rejection, error, or partial effect.

# 21. Retry, Idempotency, Concurrency, and Replay

Distributed execution systems retry, race, partition, and recover. Evidence must distinguish:

- retransmission of the same finalized record;
- reprocessing of the same request under the same idempotency contract;
- a new attempt of the same logical execution;
- a duplicate external response;
- an intentionally repeated action;
- malicious replay;
- concurrent attempts or competing Executors;
- recovery or failover after uncertain effect.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-137 — Retransmission.** Retransmission of identical canonical evidence under the same record identifier MUST NOT create a new claimed Execution Event.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-138 — New attempt.** A new attempt MUST create new event records and a distinct `attempt_id`, even when the action and `execution_id` are unchanged.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-139 — Retry relationship.** A new attempt MUST reference the prior attempt and state the retry reason, authority, Policy, idempotency expectation, and known prior effect state.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-140 — Uncertain effect.** A retry after timeout, partition, crash, or lost response MUST preserve uncertainty about prior external effect until resolved by authoritative evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-141 — Duplicate response.** Duplicate external responses MAY be deduplicated operationally, but each conflict, changed payload, changed source, or changed status MUST remain visible to evidence processing.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-142 — Concurrent attempts.** Concurrent or competing attempts MUST remain separately identifiable and MUST NOT be collapsed solely because they share an execution or idempotency identifier.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-143 — Replay defense.** Replay evaluation MUST consider target environment, principal, action, validity, nonce or idempotency state, prior attempts, external effects, and applicable Policy.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-144 — No last-write-wins.** Conflicting execution events or state claims MUST NOT be resolved solely by database update time, arrival order, or highest untrusted timestamp.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-145 — Recovery visibility.** Failover, manual recovery, compensating action, or degraded-mode continuation affecting execution MUST create or reference accountable evidence.

# 27. Finalization, Submission, and Commitment Lifecycle

Before finalization, a local draft may be revised. Finalization fixes the protected content used for digesting and signing.

```text
Draft
  │ validate and finalize
  ▼
Finalized
  │ sign
  ▼
Signed
  │ submit
  ▼
Submitted
  │ accepted by receiver under defined rules
  ▼
Accepted
  │ historical commitment
  ▼
Committed
  │ later evidence
  ├── Witnessed
  ├── Checkpointed
  ├── Anchored
  ├── Preserved
  └── Verified
```

These are lifecycle states of the Evidence Record, not statuses of the represented business execution.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-175 — Finalization boundary.** Finalization MUST establish the complete protected content before issuer proof generation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-176 — Post-finalization change.** Any change to protected content after finalization MUST create a new object or explicitly related replacement and MUST NOT retain the old digest or proofs.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-177 — Lifecycle distinction.** Draft, finalized, signed, submitted, accepted, committed, witnessed, checkpointed, anchored, preserved, and verified states MUST remain distinct.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-178 — Event/lifecycle distinction.** The represented `event_type` and `event_status` MUST NOT be confused with the lifecycle of the Execution Evidence Record itself.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-179 — Submission boundary.** A submitted or queued record MUST NOT be represented as accepted or committed merely because delivery was attempted.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-180 — Acceptance boundary.** Acceptance of the record for processing MUST NOT be represented as Commitment or as acceptance of the underlying business action.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-181 — Commitment boundary.** Commitment proves entry of the defined record or commitment into protected history and MUST NOT by itself prove event truth, Authority, execution, result, settlement, or Completeness.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-182 — Immutable history.** Finalized and especially committed content MUST NOT be silently modified; later correction, result change, reversal, or dispute requires additional accountable state.

# 29. Execution Evidence Transition Record

An Execution Evidence Transition Record adds accountable meaning about an earlier Execution Evidence Record without altering it.

For this version:

```text
object_type    = taip.execution.evidence.transition
object_version = 0.1.0
```

Initial transition types are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `correct` | Corrects a proposition in the earlier evidence statement |
| `supersede` | Replaces the earlier statement for a defined interpretive scope |
| `annotate` | Adds bounded context or supporting evidence without changing the earlier assertion |
| `dispute` | Records an attributable challenge to a defined proposition |
| `resolve_dispute` | Records a governed resolution or disposition of a prior dispute |

A reversal, refund, chargeback, or compensating action is an operational event and uses a new Execution Evidence Record with an explicit relationship to the original action.

The transition logical model contains `transition_id`, `transition_type`, strong `target_evidence`, optional strong `replacement_evidence`, issuer, principal, Authority, Policy, effective time, disputed or corrected proposition, reason, relationships, extensions, canonicalization, and proofs.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-189 — Append-only transition.** A transition MUST create a new independently identifiable protected object and MUST NOT modify the target record.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-190 — Strong transition target.** `target_evidence` MUST bind the target's identifier, object type, object version, canonical digest, digest algorithm, and representation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-191 — Transition Authority.** The transition issuer MUST provide Authority and Policy context sufficient to evaluate its right and role to issue the claimed transition.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-192 — Proposition scope.** A correction, annotation, dispute, or resolution MUST identify the exact protected proposition or result dimension affected.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-193 — Supersession replacement.** `supersede` MUST bind exactly one replacement record and define the affected interpretive scope and effective boundary.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-194 — Correction boundary.** A transition MUST NOT silently change the historical event, issuer, action, operation, attempt, or source; a materially different event requires a new primary Execution Evidence Record.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-195 — Reversal separation.** A transition MUST NOT be used to represent a real-world reversal or compensating action that itself requires Execution Evidence.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-196 — Non-retroactivity.** Correction, supersession, annotation, dispute, or resolution MUST NOT erase the fact that the earlier assertion was issued.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-197 — Transition conflict.** Conflicting, cyclic, unauthorized, or non-orderable transitions MUST remain visible and MUST produce a bounded state result.

# 30. Validation Procedure

A TAIP-0003 validator evaluates the record in ordered dimensions. Later success does not erase an earlier failure.

## 30.1 Parsing and Binding

Confirm the declared binding, media type, encoding, duplicate-property rules, size limits, and object boundaries.

## 30.2 Structural Validation

Confirm required properties, cardinalities, types, event-specific fields, status combinations, and extension placement.

## 30.3 Identity and Action Validation

Evaluate record, execution, attempt, sequence, idempotency, action, component, and external-operation identifier semantics.

## 30.4 Causal and Semantic Validation

Evaluate event type, status, action, predecessors, inputs, outputs, result, error, effects, and relationship compatibility.

## 30.5 Canonical and Cryptographic Validation

Reproduce canonical bytes and digest, then validate proofs, algorithms, Key Purpose, and Historical Key State.

## 30.6 Authority and Policy Validation

Evaluate request, execution, reporting, result, and settlement Authority; delegation; approvals; Policy; exceptions; and effective boundaries.

## 30.7 Temporal, Retry, and Ordering Validation

Evaluate time meanings, attempt relationships, idempotency, concurrency, replay, external ordering, and uncertainty.

## 30.8 Lifecycle and Transition Validation

Evaluate finalization, submission, Commitment evidence, duplicates, corrections, disputes, reversals, and state reconstruction.

## 30.9 Profile and Completeness Validation

Evaluate all controls and dependencies required by the selected Trust Profile and Accountability Claim.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-198 — Layered validation.** Validation MUST preserve separate results for parsing, structure, identifiers, action, causality, semantics, result, error, canonicalization, cryptography, identity, Authority, Policy, time, retry, lifecycle, profile, and completeness.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-199 — Validation order.** An implementation MUST NOT make semantic or cryptographic success claims when prerequisite parsing or canonicalization failed.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-200 — Event-specific validation.** A validator MUST apply the exact event-type, status, action, result, error, and transition rules identified by the protected versions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-201 — Historical dependencies.** Validation MUST use the key, Authority, Policy, schema, event Registry, external mapping, profile, and transition state applicable at the evaluated boundary.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-202 — Unknown critical semantics.** Unsupported critical events, statuses, actions, relationships, results, errors, extensions, algorithms, or versions MUST produce Unsupported for affected conclusions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-203 — Resource controls.** Parsers and resolvers MUST enforce governed input, nesting, collection, graph-depth, reference-count, retrieval-size, decompression, and processing-time limits.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-204 — No silent repair.** A validator MUST NOT silently normalize material ambiguity, invent a missing field, select conflicting evidence, fill unknown effect, or repair protected content.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-205 — Reproducible inputs.** A validation result MUST identify the exact record and dependency digests, artifact versions, Verification Time, and evaluation configuration used.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-206 — Result limitation.** Successful object validation MUST NOT be represented as proof that the underlying event or result occurred, was authorized, or was complete.

# 31. Execution Evidence Verification Result

A verifier emits dimensioned results rather than one overloaded boolean.

Initial result values are:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Pass` | Available evidence satisfies the evaluated rule |
| `Fail` | Available evidence contradicts or violates the evaluated rule |
| `Indeterminate` | Evidence is insufficient, unavailable, conflicted, or non-orderable |
| `Unsupported` | Required semantics or algorithms are not supported |
| `NotApplicable` | The rule does not apply under the explicit context |

A Verification result should include:

- result identifier and Verification Time;
- verifier identity and implementation version where attributable;
- Execution Evidence, execution, attempt, and external-operation identifiers;
- exact record, predecessor, payload, and dependency digests;
- Verification Context and requested Accountability Claims;
- normative artifact, profile, Registry, mapping, and algorithm versions;
- dimensioned findings;
- effective execution state if evaluated;
- missing, unavailable, redacted, encrypted, conflicted, or unsupported dependencies;
- warnings, assumptions, and non-protocol limits.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-207 — Dimensioned result.** A verifier MUST report findings by validation dimension and MUST NOT collapse materially different failures into one generic invalid status.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-208 — Bounded conclusion.** A Verification conclusion MUST state exactly whether it concerns object integrity, issuer attribution, causal binding, Authority, Policy, event occurrence, result, settlement, historical state, effective state, profile satisfaction, or another defined claim.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-209 — Missing evidence.** Missing, unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, conflicted, expired, revoked, or unsupported evidence MUST remain visible in the result.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-210 — No current-state substitution.** A verifier MUST NOT replace unavailable Historical State with current key, Authority, Policy, mapping, profile, or lifecycle state without explicit limitation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-211 — Deterministic composition.** Result precedence and composition MUST be defined so independent conforming verifiers given the same inputs and context reach compatible protocol conclusions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-212 — Verification boundary.** A valid Verification Report is evidence of an evaluation under a defined context; it is not automatic proof of real-world occurrence, business truth, legal effect, or evidence Completeness beyond that context.

# 32. Portability, Packaging, and Preservation

Execution Evidence must remain independently interpretable outside the producer's application.

A portable package may contain:

- Execution Evidence Records and transitions;
- related Intent and Acceptance Records;
- Authorization, approval, Authority, Policy, and evaluation evidence;
- schemas, event and status definitions, external mappings, and Canonical Representation material;
- identity, key, and Historical Key State evidence;
- inputs, outputs, receipts, and external payloads;
- retry, component, batch, reversal, and reconciliation evidence;
- Commitment, Witness, Checkpoint, anchor, and Preservation evidence;
- Trust Profile and Registry dependencies;
- a manifest identifying included, external, unavailable, or withheld material.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-213 — Portable interpretation.** A conforming export MUST preserve protected Execution Evidence semantics without requiring access to the producer's private database or proprietary user interface.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-214 — Dependency manifest.** A package MUST identify every dependency required for the claimed Verification result and its availability, integrity, version, and disclosure status.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-215 — Historical material.** Long-term preservation MUST retain or integrity-bind records, transitions, inputs, outputs, schemas, mappings, keys, Authority, Policy, profile, and specification versions required for future interpretation.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-216 — Package boundary.** Valid package structure or package integrity MUST NOT be interpreted as event truth, result correctness, settlement finality, evidence Completeness, or Trust Profile achievement.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-217 — Unavailable dependency.** A preserved package MUST identify dependencies that are external, unavailable, withheld, redacted, encrypted, unsupported, or not evaluated.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-218 — Migration relationship.** A migrated representation MUST bind source and target digests, transformation method, tool and version, authority, time, validation result, and information loss.

# 33. Conformance

TAIP-0003 conformance is role-specific.

## 33.1 Producer Conformance

A conforming producer creates complete logical objects, assigns distinct record, execution, and attempt identifiers, binds action and causality, applies event schemas, finalizes protected content, and emits issuer proofs.

## 33.2 Parser Conformance

A conforming parser recognizes the binding and exposes every protected logical property without silent coercion or loss.

## 33.3 Validator Conformance

A conforming validator evaluates structural, identity, action, causal, semantic, result, error, canonicalization, cryptographic, Authority, Policy, temporal, retry, relationship, and lifecycle rules within its declared capability scope.

## 33.4 State Evaluator Conformance

A conforming state evaluator reconstructs effective execution state using validated events, transitions, relationships, state-machine rules, and ordering evidence without last-write-wins shortcuts.

## 33.5 Transition Producer Conformance

A conforming transition producer creates append-only, strongly target-bound, authorized correction, supersession, annotation, or dispute evidence.

## 33.6 Verifier Conformance

A conforming verifier evaluates available evidence under an explicit context and emits dimensioned bounded results.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-219 — Declared role.** Every TAIP-0003 conformance claim MUST identify the implemented role and exact specification, schema, event Registry, status Registry, canonicalization, mapping, profile, binding, and test-suite versions.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-220 — Positive tests.** Tests MUST cover every supported event type, permitted status, action schema, source class, Authority status, Policy status, result form, error form, partial outcome, retry form, transition type, and proof suite.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-221 — Negative tests.** Tests MUST cover missing fields, identifier collisions, weak predecessors, invalid event/status combinations, ambiguous amounts, false finality, hidden partial failure, invalid canonicalization, invalid proofs, Authority failure, Policy failure, time errors, replay, conflicts, and resource limits.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-222 — Boundary tests.** Tests MUST distinguish request, receipt, acceptance, start, completion, result, settlement, reversal, evidence Acceptance, Commitment, current state, and business truth.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-223 — Distributed-system tests.** Tests MUST cover retransmission, new attempts, timeouts with uncertain effect, duplicate external responses, idempotency collisions, concurrency, failover, delayed reconciliation, and non-orderable events.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-224 — Cross-implementation tests.** Canonicalization, digest, Signature input, exact-number handling, identifiers, causal binding, temporal evaluation, state reconstruction, and result composition MUST be tested across independent implementations before stable release.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-225 — Test traceability.** Every normative `TAIP-0003-REQ-*` rule MUST map to one or more test assertions or an explicit justified non-testable disposition.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-226 — Scoped report.** Passing a parser or producer subset MUST NOT be represented as full TAIP-0003 verifier or deployment conformance.

**TAIP-0003-REQ-227 — Report evidence.** A conformance report MUST identify passed, failed, skipped, unsupported, and not-applicable tests together with environment, configuration, implementation build, and known limitations.

# 37. Security Considerations

## 37.1 Forged Execution Evidence

An attacker may create or alter a record to make an action appear requested, executed, failed, or settled by another party. Protected issuer identity, deterministic canonicalization, Signatures, Historical Key State, Authority evaluation, and strong dependencies are required defenses.

## 37.2 Source Misattribution

An intermediary may report an external response as though it came directly from the execution or settlement authority. Records must preserve source-of-knowledge, external receipts, issuer identity, and role boundaries.

## 37.3 Request or Action Substitution

An attacker may substitute an action sharing a workflow ID, display summary, amount, or external identifier. Strong references and exact protected action comparison are required.

## 37.4 Status Inflation

Receipt may be relabeled Acceptance, completion may be relabeled settlement, or settlement may be relabeled irreversible. Governed event, status, result, source, and finality semantics must prevent this escalation.

## 37.5 Replay and Duplicate Effect

Replaying valid requests or events may cause duplicate effects. Operation, attempt, idempotency, environment, time, action, and prior-effect evidence must be evaluated together.

## 37.6 Timeout Ambiguity

A lost response may cause a requester to retry an operation that already took effect. Evidence must preserve uncertain effect and support authoritative resolution before unsafe retry where required by Policy.

## 37.7 Partial Failure Masking

Aggregate success may hide failed, pending, or duplicated components. Protected component outcomes and explicit aggregation rules are required.

## 37.8 External Identifier and Receipt Substitution

External identifiers may collide, be reused, refer to another environment, or be paired with substituted receipts. Namespace, issuer, environment, digest, representation, and source validation are required.

## 37.9 Time and Ordering Manipulation

Producer clocks, external timestamps, arrival order, and local sequence can be manipulated or misunderstood. Stronger ordering claims require causal, Chain, Witness, Checkpoint, or authoritative source evidence.

## 37.10 Parser Differential

Duplicate fields, Unicode normalization, exact-decimal handling, time parsing, array ordering, unknown properties, and extension behavior may create cross-implementation disagreement. Stable bindings require deterministic rejection and test vectors.

## 37.11 Error Oracle and Sensitive State Leakage

Detailed rejection, fraud, liquidity, counterparty, and Policy information may turn an execution interface into a sensitive-state oracle. Disclosure rules and rate, access, and audit controls should limit exposure.

## 37.12 Overclaiming

The central semantic attack is presenting valid signed Execution Evidence as indisputable real-world truth, Authorization, settlement finality, historical Commitment, completeness, or legal correctness. Verifiers and user interfaces must preserve the bounded claim.

# 38. Privacy Considerations

Execution Evidence may expose amounts, accounts, assets, counterparties, beneficiaries, timing, locations, identifiers, commercial relationships, retry behavior, system failures, liquidity, fraud controls, Policy, and organizational structure.

Implementations should minimize exposure through:

- commitments or digests instead of unnecessary plaintext;
- separated encrypted payloads;
- selective disclosure of action, result, and error fields;
- role-limited access;
- privacy-aware identity and correlation references;
- minimized external-system and endpoint metadata;
- disclosure-specific Dispute Packs;
- protected resolver requests;
- retention appropriate to the claim and Trust Profile.

Even encrypted payloads can leave sensitive metadata visible. Event type, status, issuer, execution timing, retry count, external route, payload size, error category, and relationship graph can reveal behavior.

Privacy restrictions do not authorize silent completeness. A verifier unable to access required information must report the limitation.

---

# 39. Operational Considerations

Implementations must account for:

- synchronous, asynchronous, offline, and delayed evidence creation;
- long-running and multi-stage operations;
- duplicate delivery and idempotent processing;
- timeouts with uncertain external effect;
- network partitions, failover, and split-brain execution;
- partial, batch, and component outcomes;
- cancellation, retry, reversal, and reconciliation races;
- external-system identifier reuse and environment confusion;
- resolver, identity, key, Policy, Registry, and Commitment outages;
- clock skew and non-orderable events;
- key rotation between event, record, and Verification;
- long-term preservation of corrected, disputed, reversed, or superseded evidence;
- user-interface wording that does not overstate status, result, finality, or trust.

A system may stop, reject, retry, queue, defer, continue, compensate, or require manual review according to its Authority, Policy, and Trust Profile. The operational decision, event actually reported, and assurance actually achieved must remain explicit.

Operational logs and metrics may support diagnosis but do not replace protected Execution Evidence or its historical lifecycle evidence.

# 43. Summary

TAIP-0003 defines portable and attributable evidence for individual execution-related events.

An Execution Evidence Record identifies:

- the primary event, status, issuer, principal, and source of knowledge;
- the logical execution, attempt, sequence, and idempotency context;
- the exact action, causal predecessors, Authority, and Policy context;
- inputs, outputs, result, error, external operation, time, and effect scope;
- Trust Profile, confidentiality, Canonical Representation, and proof semantics.

An Execution Evidence Transition Record adds correction, supersession, annotation, or dispute state without rewriting earlier evidence. Operational reversal remains a new Execution Event.

The central boundary is:

```text
Requested
≠ Received
≠ Accepted for Processing
≠ Executed
≠ Completed
≠ Result Confirmed
≠ Settled
≠ Irreversible
≠ Committed as Evidence
```

TAIP-0003 makes an execution history independently inspectable without pretending that one producer observed or proved every stage.

The next specification, `TAIP-0004`, defines Independent Witness evidence for observing specified objects, events, commitments, or historical boundaries across a materially evaluated trust boundary.
